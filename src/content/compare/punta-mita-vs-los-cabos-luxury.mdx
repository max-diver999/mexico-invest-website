## Price architecture and entry tickets

Los Cabos offers a wider capital ladder, critical for investors who want Pacific exposure without $2M minimums. Punta Mita is effectively $1M-plus across the peninsula, while Los Cabos runs from roughly $350,000 for investor-grade condos to $10M-plus on branded villas. That ladder matters because it decides whether you can build a position gradually or must commit at the top. The practical consequence of that ladder: Los Cabos lets a buyer build a position over several years and several price points, while Punta Mita requires committing at the top on the first purchase. For a buyer who wants Pacific luxury exposure but not a seven-figure single bet, that difference decides the market before any yield comparison does.

## Yield reality: lifestyle assets, not cash machines

Luxury buyers who demand 8% net should look at Playa del Carmen Centro (~4.4% net), not Punta Mita or Quivira. Both markets net 3–5% after the fee stack, and in both the fees rather than the rates are what compress it: brand licence, club dues, FF&E reserves and heavy HOAs. Neither is a cash-flow investment, and pricing them as one is the recurring error at this level. Neither is a cash-flow asset, and the fee stack rather than the nightly rate is why: brand licence, club dues, FF&E reserves and a heavy HOA all sit between gross and net. A buyer who needs 6%-plus should be in Playa del Carmen, where the same capital buys three units instead of part of one.

## Punta Mita advantages

Punta Mita's master-planned gated resort with Four Seasons, St. Regis, and championship golf creates Mexico's most supply-constrained ultra-luxury micro-market, 200+ listings in 2026 still represent limited comparable inventory versus open Cabos corridors.

Gated scarcity: Single resort peninsula, UHNW buyer pool, international recognition.

Brand stack: Four Seasons, St. Regis, Montage $5M–$18.5M (2027), Pendry 2026 (32 homes).

Lifestyle thesis: Golf, yacht club, private beaches, trophy asset category.

Nayarit growth tailwind: State +12.52% price growth 2025, second-fastest tracked behind Quintana Roo +14.68%.

Resale within gates: Strongest for established Four Seasons/St. Regis product.

Lower party noise than Cabo San Lucas marina zone, residential resort calm.

Projects: [Montage Punta Mita](/projects/montage-punta-mita/) · [Four Seasons Punta Mita](/projects/four-seasons-punta-mita/).

## Los Cabos advantages

Los Cabos delivers deeper US flight connectivity, broader price ladder, and 2026 negotiation leverage after Q1 volume fell 29.7% YoY while average sales held near $809K, mature luxury with buyer power. The negotiation window is the concrete one: on listings past 120 days, a buyer can currently ask for price, seller-paid closing or furnishings in a way that was not available in 2022. Beyond the ladder and the flights, Los Cabos has depth: more transactions, more comparable sales, more managers and more brokers who have done this before. In an ultra-luxury market that information advantage is worth real money at both ends of a hold.

Flight map: SJD direct LAX, SFO, SAN, SEA, PHX, DFW, IAH, California second-home culture.

Price ladder: $350K+ investor 1BR to $13.5M branded, diversify within one market.

2026 negotiation: Volume -29.7% YoY Q1; inventory broader; sellers flexible.

Sub-market choice: Cabo San Lucas marina tourism, San José arts walkability, Corridor branded, East Cape growth.

Quivira liquidity: Copala/Mavila resale depth, most liquid branded Cabos segment.

Insurance narrative: Pacific hurricane exposure lower than Quintana Roo, still insurable, not zero risk.

Areas: [Cabo San Lucas](/areas/cabo-san-lucas/) · [San José del Cabo](/areas/san-jose-del-cabo/) · [Cabo Corridor](/areas/cabo-corridor/).

## Operational and STR differences

Branded buildings on both coasts commonly require enrolment in the operator's rental programme rather than permitting independent listing, and that changes the instrument: a 30% to 50% share of net room revenue is not the same as a 25% management fee on gross. Verify the HOA and programme rules before the offer, not after.

| Element | Punta Mita | Los Cabos |
|---|---|---|
| Guest profile | UHNW, corporate retreats | Luxury + owner-use heavy |
| STR management | Hotel programs common | HOA rules dominate |
| Minimum stay | Often weekly+ | Mixed |
| Car dependency | High (gated) | High outside San José |
| Rental registration | Nayarit medium | BCS medium |

STR: verify HOA before offer, many branded buildings restrict independent Airbnb.

## Tax and ownership (both)

Fideicomiso 50-year renewable trust. ISR withholding on sale, 25% gross or 35% net methods. US owners: Schedule E if renting; not a tax haven. The ownership mechanics are identical on both peninsulas, so nothing in the legal stack differentiates them. What differs is the fee stack above it, which is where the real cost comparison lives. Because the legal stack is identical, everything that distinguishes these two markets is commercial: fee levels, rental-programme terms, guest demand and the depth of the resale pool. There is no structuring advantage on either peninsula.

## Decision framework

1. Ticket size: under $1M Pacific → Cabos only.
2. Flight origin: west coast heavy → Cabos; Nayarit lifestyle → Punta Mita.
3. Yield vs trophy: both sub-5% net luxury; underwrite owner-use value.
4. Liquidity: Quivira Copala/Mavila vs Punta Surf Club resale depth.
5. 2026 timing: Cabos volume dip vs Punta rebalancing, both favor patient DD.

## Bottom line

Punta Mita is the gated ultra-luxury peninsula, fewer deals, higher tickets, Montage/Pendry/Four Seasons ecosystem, 3–5% net lifestyle math. Los Cabos is the deeper, more liquid market with a wider price ladder and better US flight connectivity; Punta Mita is the smaller, more exclusive one with a correspondingly narrower exit. Both are lifestyle assets first. A buyer choosing between them is really choosing between depth and exclusivity. Los Cabos gives you options, comparables and an exit; Punta Mita gives you seclusion and a smaller, more durable buyer pool. Neither answer is wrong, but they are not interchangeable and the yield tables will not tell you which you want.

Los Cabos is the full-spectrum Pacific luxury market, $350K to $13.5M, deeper flights, Q1 -29.7% volume negotiation, Quivira liquidity.

## Negotiation tactics 2026

Los Cabos: Reference Q1 -29.7% volume YoY, ask for seller-paid closing, furniture, or price reductions on listings over 120 days. Punta Mita: fewer listings means less leverage, so the ask is usually furnishings, club transfer fees or a delivery credit rather than headline price.

Punta Mita: Target dated inventory in the 200+ listing pool; new Montage/Pendry phases price at premium, resale comps matter more than launch brochures.

## Currency and USD denomination

Both coasts price luxury in USD for foreign buyers, peso moves affect local buyers more than offshore cash. Wire large tickets through FX specialists; on $2M+ closings, a 1% bank spread costs $20,000, more than annual fideicomiso fees.

## Professional network and vendor depth

Los Cabos carries deeper US-facing property management, insurance, and legal benches after decades of California-Texas second-home volume. Punta Mita's gated ecosystem centralizes services, convenient until you need off-resort pricing benchmarks. Interview two independent PM firms in either market before trusting branded rental projections; ultra-luxury guest expectations raise turnover costs faster than mid-market Playa grids.
