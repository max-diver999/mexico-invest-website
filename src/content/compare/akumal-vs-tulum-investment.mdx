## Yield deep dive

Akumal's family demographic supports longer stays and lower turnover costs. Tulum's gross marketing looks competitive until HOA and identical-unit competition compress net, especially Region 15. The practical figures: Akumal at 3.5–4% net on 58–68% annual occupancy against Region 15 at 2.6–3% on a lower entry price. Akumal wins on net despite costing more, and the reason is competition density rather than rate.

Akumal's 2BR row is the standout and it is worth understanding why. At 6.5% gross and 5.0% net, the gap between the two is only 150 basis points, against 310 in Aldea Zama and 340 in Region 15 on similar gross. Family guests taking week-long stays produce far fewer turnovers, so cleaning and turnover cost falls, and Akumal's small inventory means no cluster of identical units racing rates downward. The lesson generalises: in Mexican coastal markets the gross-to-net gap is set by competition density and turnover frequency, not by the nightly rate.

| Market / product | Gross (indicative) | Net |
|---|---:|---:|
| Akumal 2BR condo | 6.5% | 5.0% |
| Akumal 1BR | 6.0% | 4.6% |
| Tulum Aldea Zama 1BR | 6.5% | 3.4% |
| Tulum Region 15 1BR | 6.0% | 2.6% |
| Tulum La Veleta | 6.5% | 3.3% |
| Playa Centro (benchmark) | 6.6% | 4.4% |

## Entry price and all-In cost

Tulum's lower sticker attracts capital, but closing stack and weak resale can erase the advantage. Closing runs about 8% in both towns, so a $60,000 lower Tulum sticker saves roughly $65,000 all-in — real money, until a 74-day resale and a sub-3% net take it back over a five-year hold.

Carrying the sticker saving through a five-year hold is what reverses the comparison. A $60,000 lower Tulum entry saves roughly $65,000 all in once the 8% closing stack is applied, which is genuine money. Against that, Region 15 at 2.6% net on $225,000 produces about $5,850 a year while an Akumal 2BR at 5.0% on $350,000 produces $17,500 — a $11,650 annual gap, or $58,250 across five years, before the 74-day Tulum DOM and thinner resale bid are counted. The cheaper entry is spent by year five.

## Akumal advantages

Akumal's protected turtle sanctuary creates defensible family tourism demand that tower developers cannot replicate with amenities alone, limited supply supports pricing resilience even when national foreign purchases exceed 40,000 annually. There is no tower pipeline here and no realistic prospect of one: while Tulum absorbed a fivefold increase in short-term rental supply between 2020 and 2026, Akumal absorbed almost none.

Family STR niche: Calm bay snorkeling, turtle encounters, and eco-tourism attract multi-generational guests willing to pay premiums for safe water and relaxed atmosphere versus Playa party zones.

Supply constraint: Fewer identical-unit towers than Tulum Region 15. Less ADR compression from walkable competition.

Established infrastructure: Medical clinic, paved roads, dive shops, resort services, more predictable than jungle fringe Tulum parcels.

Net yield on 2BR: ~5.0% indicative with family stay length reducing turnover.

Resale psychology: Buyers seeking quiet bay lifestyle, smaller but durable demand pool.

Distance: ~45 min north of Tulum, ~90 min from CUN, acceptable for family arrivals.

Compare north: [Puerto Morelos](/areas/puerto-morelos/) shares "quieter RM" thesis.

## Tulum advantages

Tulum offers global brand recognition, lower capital entry, and upside in differentiated eco-luxury product, but only when colonia and building selection avoid Region 15 traps. Aldea Zama holds near 3.4% net and beach-zone niches hold their premium; Region 15 does neither. Buying "Tulum" without specifying which of those you mean is how most disappointing outcomes in this market started.

Lower entry: Fringe zones from ~$150K; median ~$285K, capital efficient for optionality plays.

Brand premium: Wellness, design, nomad culture command ADR in differentiated units.

Infrastructure tailwinds: Tren Maya operational; Felipe Carrillo Puerto airport; FIFA 2026 spillover expected.

Aldea Zama grid: Master-planned community with ~3.4% net, core investable Tulum versus Region 15.

Upside optionality: Selective pre-construction in compliant buildings; see [Aggressive Investor Tulum](/guides/aggressive-investor-tulum-precon/).

Red flag: Three-year inventory high + 74-day DOM = negotiate hard or walk.

## Due diligence differences

Akumal: Verify beach access rights, turtle-zone regulations, resort HOA rental pools versus independent STR. Tulum: verify environmental clearance on cenote-adjacent parcels, ejido boundary distance on the survey, and how many identical floor plans in your own building already sit on the platforms. The two towns fail in different places, so the same checklist does not cover both.

Tulum: SEDETUS compliance, building permit status, HOA STR bylaws, competition mapping within 1 km, net statements not pro-forma.

Shared: Fideicomiso via authorized bank, libertad de gravamen, predial current, independent lawyer, never seller-only counsel.

## Tax and compliance (both)

US owners report rent on Schedule E. Mexico SAT increased platform reporting scrutiny in 2026. Municipal registration required where enforced. Both sit in Solidaridad-adjacent Quintana Roo, so the state lodging tax and the SAT stack are identical; what differs is enforcement intensity, which has been heavier in Tulum since 2024 simply because there is more inventory to police.

Not a tax haven, budget CPA: [US Taxes Mexico Rental](/guides/us-taxes-mexico-rental-property/).

## Decision framework

1. Define guest: families (Akumal) vs nomads/wellness (Tulum selective).
2. Model net: use colonia tables, not city averages.
3. Check supply: Tulum inventory at 3-year high favors buyers; Akumal scarcity favors sellers on prime bay.
4. Compare Playa: if volume STR is core, [Playa del Carmen](/areas/playa-del-carmen/) may beat both on liquidity.
5. Run all-in math: 5–10% closing on entry price.

## Bottom line

Akumal and Tulum share a highway but not a thesis. Akumal is the family eco-niche with supply constraint and stronger 2BR net yields. Tulum is the global brand with lower entry and higher execution risk in 2026's oversupplied towers.

Quintana Roo's macro strength, +14.68% 2025 growth, 40,000+ foreign deals nationally, supports the corridor. Colonia selection still determines whether you capture that macro or subsidize another owner's exit from Region 15.

## Financing and cash buyer norms

Foreign coastal purchases nationally remain cash-heavy, industry estimates cite 70%+ of foreign deals without Mexican bank financing. Akumal's $300K–800K band and Tulum's $150K–285K median attract wire transfers from US accounts; budget 0.3–0.8% FX specialist spread versus 1.5–3% retail bank on $300K+ transfers.

## Insurance and hurricane underwriting

Both sit on Quintana Roo's Atlantic coast, hurricane exposure is real, not theoretical. Insurance pricing differs from Los Cabos Pacific narratives but remains an operating line that compresses net yield. Budget $1,500–4,000 a year on either side, and check the named-storm deductible, which is a percentage of insured value rather than a fixed sum. Neither town gets a discount for being the quieter one.

- Windstorm coverage, verify building policy and unit rider
- STR guest liability, required for professional operators
- Builder warranty on pre-con Tulum, verify insurer acceptance

## Infrastructure timeline comparison

Tulum received the infrastructure investment — a Tren Maya station and an airport — and Akumal received the regional benefit without the construction. That sounds like a Tulum advantage and has largely not been one: better access raised land values and supply faster than it raised rents, which is precisely how a market ends up with 74-day days-on-market. Akumal's established water, power and road services are unglamorous and already work.

| Project | Akumal impact | Tulum impact |
|---|---|---|
| Tren Maya | Regional access | Station + airport |
| Highway 307 | Primary artery | Primary artery |
| CEN infrastructure | Established | Variable by colonia |
| Municipal water/sewer | Resort-grade | Region 15 stress |

Akumal benefits from regional connectivity without depending on it for occupancy. Tulum sellers may overprice "Tren Maya premium" on weak towers, use [Tren Maya Property Impact](/news/tren-maya-property-impact/) context when negotiating.

## Record-keeping for US owners (both markets)

Rent in either town → Schedule E. Sell → ISR withholding. Mexican bank accounts over $10,000 aggregate may trigger FBAR. The file that matters most in both towns is the CFDI trail: without documented cost basis, ISR at sale is withheld at 25% of the gross price rather than 35% of actual gain, and on a modest appreciation that is the larger number.

Akumal family STR often shows cleaner seasonal patterns; Tulum nomad STR may mix currencies and platforms, SAT 2026 platform reporting intensifies documentation requirements.

## Final selector: three questions

1. Can you underwrite 40 competing units within 1 km? If no → Akumal or Playa, not Tulum Region 15.
2. Is your guest a family with kids snorkeling? If yes → Akumal thesis strengthens.
3. Do you need lowest ticket? Tulum fringe: only with lawyer and rental proof.

Wrong answer pattern: buying Tulum because Quintana Roo grew +14.68% state-wide while ignoring 74-day DOM and 2.6% net on generic inventory. A state-level growth figure averages Cancún, Playa, Akumal and Tulum together and describes none of them. The three questions below are building-level and settle the choice without reference to any state statistic.

Akumal's smaller market has fewer agents but also less churn; Tulum's hype cycle attracts aggressive promoters, verify track record on delivered buildings, not launch renderings.
