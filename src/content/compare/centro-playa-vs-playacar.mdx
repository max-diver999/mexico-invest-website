Playa del Carmen splits between urban walkable Centro and master-planned Playacar, 729 acres with Robert Trent Jones golf, beach club, and gated security. Investors often conflate "Playa", colonia choice drives 100+ bps net yield swing. This comparison runs numbers, personas, and risks.

## Head-To-head comparison table

Centro Playa del Carmen delivers superior net yields through lower HOA bands and walkable STR operations that reduce guest friction and management costs. Playacar trades yield for luxury infrastructure, golf, beach club, gated security, attracting premium guests and family lifestyle buyers at higher capital entry and ongoing fee load.

## HOA cost anatomy

Centro HOA covers building maintenance, pool, security, $150–450 typical. Playacar adds golf course upkeep, beach club, 24/7 gated security, landscaping at resort scale, $400–1,200+ monthly. Special assessments hit both; Playacar capex cycles larger.

Sizing that 160 basis point swing against gross revenue shows where a Playacar yield actually goes. On a $300,000 unit grossing roughly $18,600 a year, a $200 monthly Centro fee costs $2,400 and consumes 13% of gross; a $600 Playacar fee costs $7,200 and consumes 39%. The extra $4,800 buys golf course upkeep, a private beach club and a 24/7 gated perimeter, which are real amenities that guests notice and pay something for, just not $4,800 a year more. Playacar's capital expenditure cycles are also larger, so budget special assessments on top rather than treating the monthly figure as the whole cost.

| HOA component | Centro | Playacar |
|---|---|---|
| Base building | Yes | Yes |
| Golf course | No | Yes |
| Beach club | No | Yes |
| Gated perimeter | Partial | 24/7 |
| Net yield impact | Lower | −100 bps typical |

$600/month vs $200/month on $300K unit → ~160 bps net swing.

## Entry price and capital efficiency

Centro investor 1BR $310K at 4.4% net = $13,640 annual. Playacar $450K at 3.4% net = $15,300, higher absolute dollars but 3.4% return on more capital. Yield-focused: Centro capital efficiency wins. Lifestyle-focused: Playacar amenity bundle may justify spread.

Deploying the difference is what settles the argument for a yield buyer. A Playacar 1BR at $450,000 nets $15,300 a year at 3.4%; a Centro 1BR at $310,000 nets $13,640 at 4.4%, leaving $140,000 unspent. That residual, put into a second Gonzalo Guerrero unit at $200,000 to $240,000 with modest leverage or held against the next cycle, is worth more than the $1,660 of extra annual income Playacar delivers. The gated amenity bundle is a lifestyle purchase priced at roughly $140,000 of foregone capital efficiency, which is a defensible choice made explicitly and a poor one made by accident.

## How does this comparison stack up for Mexico investors?

Families prioritise Playacar security, golf, beach club, often hybrid personal use + rent. Pure investors prioritise Centro net and liquidity. Resale: family buyers hunt Playacar; operators hunt Centro, know your exit buyer. The split is clean: Centro is the investor market and Playacar is the family and owner-user market, and they resell to different buyers. Centro nets more per dollar; Playacar holds value better with a hybrid personal-use thesis.

## Security and perception

Playacar gated community signals security premium for family buyers, 24/7 controlled access. Centro urban, verify building security, street lighting, specific block. Both established Playa markets, stick to known colonias, not fringe. The commercial point is not which is safer but which the guest perceives as safer, a gated entrance sells itself to a family in a way that a well-lit Centro street cannot, regardless of what the crime statistics say.

---

## Beach access comparison

Centro, walkable beach access varies by block distance. Playacar, private beach club model, controlled access, less public congestion. Guest marketing: "steps to beach" Centro vs "exclusive beach club" Playacar. That difference drives listing copy and guest expectation: "two blocks to the beach" in Centro is verifiable and competitive, while Playacar's beach club model is exclusive and requires explaining access rules to every guest before arrival.

---

## Golf course premium

Robert Trent Jones 18-hole championship, Playacar exclusive. Supports ADR premium golf season (Nov–Apr). Centro no golf, different comp set entirely. Non-golf investors should not pay Playacar golf HOA without capturing guest premium.

---

## STR municipal compliance

Solidaridad (Playa) requires STR registration and tax compliance, applies both zones. Some Playacar phases restrict STR, verify building bylaws before pure rental thesis. Centro STR-friendly buildings more common. Playacar is where the building rule bites hardest: several phases restrict nightly letting outright, so a pure rental thesis there needs written confirmation from the specific phase before any deposit.

## Management ecosystem

Centro: deepest property manager pool in Riviera Maya, competitive fees 25–30%. Playacar: luxury operators familiar with golf/beach club protocols, may charge premium. First-time remote owners: Centro lower mistake cost. Fee competition follows manager density, so Centro runs 25–30% and Playacar runs at the top of that band or above. A first-time remote owner is better served by Centro's depth of choice than by Playacar's specialist few.

## Worked capital: $400k deployment

Playacar: $450K golf-view + $35K closing, exceeds budget unless leverage. At $400K Playacar entry (older phase), net ~$13,600 at 3.4%, similar dollars, higher HOA risk.

## What risks should buyers plan for before they commit?

Centro: Generic tower competition, aging building assessments, STR municipal tightening, nightlife noise complaints in reviews. Playacar: HOA restrictions on rentals, higher fixed carrying costs, and a narrower resale pool that reacts more sharply to a soft luxury cycle. The two colonias fail in opposite directions.

Playacar: HOA increases, luxury soft market, STR restrictions in select phases, hurricane club closure periods.

Neither: ejido, both established private title markets.

## 12-month operator timeline

Centro: Close → furnish 3 weeks → live month 2 → 68% occupancy month 4 → net 4%+ month 6. Playacar runs slower on every step, furnishing to a higher standard, HOA approval for guest access, and a longer seasoning period before the rate holds. Plan for revenue in month four rather than month two.

Playacar: Close → furnish premium 6 weeks → golf/beach club orientation → slower ramp → net 3.4% month 8–10.

## What checklist should run before you sign?

Centro Playa buildings face higher municipal visibility on STR listings; Playacar gated phases often enforce guest registration and quiet hours more strictly through HOA. That asymmetry decides which questions matter: in Centro, verify the municipal registration path and the noise profile; in Playacar, verify the phase bylaws and the guest registration procedure.

| Factor | Centro Playa | Playacar |
|---|---|---|
| STR listing scrutiny | Higher (dense tourism) | Moderate (gated phases) |
| Typical HOA | $0.35–$0.55/sq ft/mo | $0.45–$0.70/sq ft/mo |
| Parking for guests | Limited on side streets | Controlled gate access |
| Resale buyer pool | Investors + lifestyle | Families + retirees |

Verify current HOA minutes (last 12 months) for anti-STR votes before assuming Playacar is automatically STR-friendly. See [HOA Fees Mexico Condo](/guides/hoa-fees-mexico-condo/) and [Conservative Investor Mexico Playa](/guides/conservative-investor-mexico-playa/).
