## Head-To-head comparison table

Puerto Vallarta delivers stronger walkable STR economics because Zona Romántica guests leave cars parked for days while Nuevo Vallarta resort towers depend on pool-and-golf amenities and often restrict independent Airbnb in favor of hotel rental programmes, net yields reflect that operational gap. Fifteen minutes apart and in different states, these two markets share an airport and almost nothing else operationally. Nuevo Vallarta is Nayarit resort inventory sold to families arriving by car; Puerto Vallarta is Jalisco city stock sold to couples who want to walk to dinner. The registration path, the attorney and the guest are all different.

## Price and yield deep dive

Marketing gross yields on both sides of the bay cluster near 6–6.5%, but Puerto Vallarta Romántica net near 4.5% on $340K basis while Nuevo Vallarta resort core often lands 3.5% after rental-pool splits and higher HOA, marina-adjacent Nuevo Vallarta can reach 4.0% with longer nautical stays. Puerto Vallarta nets more in the walkable colonias, Nuevo Vallarta carries heavier resort HOA against longer family stays that cost less per booked night to service. Compare them on net after HOA and management rather than on entry price, because the fee stacks diverge more than the tickets do.

Walkability is what the yield gap between these two actually prices. Zona Romántica at $340,000 nets 4.5%, or roughly $15,300, while a Nuevo Vallarta resort unit at $420,000 nets 3.5%, or about $14,700 — less income on $80,000 more capital. The resort side carries HOA that funds pools, gated security and beach-club access, commonly $350 or more on a 1BR, and its guests arrive with rental cars expecting parking. Nightly rates in Banderas Bay are set by the walk to dinner, not by the amenity list.

| Zone | Price (1BR) | Gross | Net |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PV Zona Romántica | $340K | 6.5% | 4.5% |
| PV Marina Vallarta | $380K | 6.2% | 4.0% |
| PV Versalles | $300K | 6.0% | 3.8% |
| Nuevo Vallarta resort core | $420K | 6.0% | 3.5% |
| Nuevo Vallarta marina | $450K | 6.2% | 4.0% |
| Flamingos / fringe | $320K | 5.8% | 3.8% |

Footnote: micro-market; verify HOA, rental pool contracts, and municipal STR rules before underwriting.

## Puerto Vallarta investment profile

Puerto Vallarta predates the Instagram condo era with established expat infrastructure, hospital access, and restaurant walkability that produces among Mexico's strongest Pacific STR review patterns, but hillside stairs, retiree-heavy buildings without STR allowance, and Jalisco municipal registration still kill naive underwriting. PV's structural advantage is a resident economy: retirees, expats and a culinary scene that fills months a pure resort market loses. That is why its shoulder season holds up and why its occupancy curve is flatter than the bay average.

### Zona Romántica (Old Town)

STR liquidity anchor, walkable dining, galleries, beach access via hills or south shore walks. Guest reviews consistently cite restaurant proximity and Malecón evenings.

- Investor fit: Walkable STR operators
- Price band: $300K–450K 1BR
- Net signal: ~4.5%
- HOA: $250–500/month typical
- Risk: Hillside access, test guest path with luggage

### Marina Vallarta and Centro fringe

Marina flights, yacht ecosystem, more car-oriented than Romántica but longer average stays.

- Net signal: ~4%
- Price: $320K–480K
- DD: STR verification essential, mixed HOA quality

### Versalles and value pockets

Local residential colonia with emerging STR, lower ADR, thinner tourist polish, value entry.

- Price: $250K–350K
- Net: ~3.8%
- Fit: Operators accepting lower ADR for basis

### When Puerto Vallarta wins the thesis

Choose Jalisco-side PV when walkability drives reviews, when net yield matters more than resort pool slides, when retiree services and healthcare proximity support long holds, or when entry below $350K matters. Romántica is the benchmark Pacific walkable STR market in Mexico.

## Nuevo Vallarta investment profile

Nuevo Vallarta captures buyers who search "Puerto Vallarta" on OTAs but land in Nayarit resort towers, golf courses, Vidanta-adjacent ecosystems, and marina tourism support family weekly rentals when HOA permits independent STR or favorable rental-pool splits. Nuevo Vallarta's advantage is product: large-format units, resort amenities and a marina, aimed at multi-generational family groups who book weeks rather than nights. The constraint is that many towers channel rentals through a hotel-style pool and prohibit independent listing.

### Marina Nuevo Vallarta

Yacht ecosystem, nautical services, waterfront dining, weekly rental potential for boating tourists with longer average stays than Romántica weekend blocks.

- Price: $400K–600K 1BR
- Net: ~3.8–4.2%
- Guest: Families, nautical itineraries

### Golf and resort core

Shared amenity expectations, competitive similar-unit STR, rental pool programmes common.

- Price: $350K–500K
- HOA: $400–700/month
- Net: 3–4%
- Risk: Hotel programme restrictions on independent Airbnb

### Flamingos and north bay fringe

Transition toward Bucerías villages, emerging inventory, variable HOA, thinner liquidity than core Nuevo Vallarta.

- Price: $280K–400K
- Net: 3.5–4.5% indicative

### When Nuevo Vallarta wins the thesis

Choose Nuevo Vallarta when your guest avatar is families wanting pools and golf, when marina weekly blocks fit your calendar, when PVR airport proximity matters for turnover, or when you prefer resort HOA amenities over cobblestone charm, always verify STR independence from rental pool first.

## State, tax, and legal differences

Puerto Vallarta sits in Jalisco; Nuevo Vallarta in Nayarit, same national fideicomiso framework for foreigners in the restricted zone, but municipal STR registration, predial processes, and notario familiarity differ. Use state-appropriate counsel; do not assume Jalisco contracts govern Nayarit closings. The Ameca river is the state line, and it changes the municipal registration, the lodging-tax counter and which bar your attorney must belong to. A process learned on one side of the bay teaches you nothing about the other.

| Topic | Puerto Vallarta (Jalisco) | Nuevo Vallarta (Nayarit) |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership (foreign coastal) | Fideicomiso | Fideicomiso |
| ISAI acquisition tax | ~2–3% indicative | ~2–3% indicative |
| STR registration | PV municipal programs | Bahía de Banderas / Nayarit |
| Legal counsel | Jalisco-savvy | Nayarit-savvy |
| Buyer confusion risk | Low if Romántica named | High, "PV address" marketing |

## STR and HOA operational comparison

Puerto Vallarta Romántica buildings range from STR-friendly boutique stacks to retiree towers that prohibit nightly rentals, zero operational yield if you skip bylaws. Nuevo Vallarta resort HOAs often push rental pool participation with revenue splits that look like gross yield in marketing but net differently on your spreadsheet. The decisive question in Nuevo Vallarta is whether independent listing is permitted at all; in Puerto Vallarta it is whether a retiree-majority assembly is moving toward a ban. Different questions, both answered in the régimen and the minutes.

Assembly composition decides the STR position in Puerto Vallarta and it varies sharply between the two sides of the bay. Many older PV towers are majority owner-occupied by retirees who vote nightly letting down once guest traffic annoys them, while Nuevo Vallarta's resort-style developments were built for rental and rarely face that risk. A $280,000 bargain with a régimen de condominio prohibition earns 0% rental yield, so read the regime and the last 24 months of minutes before agreeing a price on either side.

| Operational factor | Puerto Vallarta | Nuevo Vallarta |
|---|---|---|
| Guest car need | Low in Romántica | High |
| Restaurant walk score | High Romántica | Low, drive to dining |
| Rental pool prevalence | Lower | Higher |
| Identical-unit competition | Moderate | High resort stacks |
| Shoulder season | Culinary tourism helps | Family holidays weighted |

Municipal rules change; verify ordinance, HOA, and tax advisor before listing. Bahía de Banderas enforcement on Nayarit side is typically low-medium versus Romántica's established STR ecosystem.

A walkable short-term-rental maximiser belongs in PV's Zona Romántica, where guests reach restaurants and beach on foot and the review patterns reflect it. ## What risks should buyers plan for before they commit?

Puerto Vallarta risks include hillside access complaints, hurricane-season occupancy softness (lower than Quintana Roo but not zero), STR municipal tightening, and buying in retiree buildings without STR rights. Nuevo Vallarta risks include rental pool lock-in, confusing Jalisco marketing for Nayarit product, HOA special assessments on pools and golf, and car-dependent guest disappointment.

### Puerto Vallarta red flags

- "Walkable" listing 20 minutes from Romántica on foot
- Retiree HOA with nightly rental prohibition
- Undocumented basis at purchase, ISR shock on exit
- Ignoring stairs and humidity maintenance on hillside units

### Nuevo Vallarta red flags

- Rental pool contract with opaque net split
- STR marketed as Romántica experience, review backlash
- Special assessments on aging resort infrastructure
- Wrong-state legal counsel on Nayarit closing

## Airport and flight access (shared PVR)

Both markets feed Licenciado Gustavo Díaz Ordaz International Airport (PVR), Nuevo Vallarta often 10–20 minutes, Romántica 15–25 minutes. West-coast US origins dominate; check your city's direct routes seasonally. Airport proximity is a shared advantage, it does not resolve walkability differences for STR guests.

## Resale and liquidity

Puerto Vallarta Romántica resale benefits from global brand recognition and retiree buyer depth, wrong-building mistakes still sell to lifestyle buyers if HOA is clean. Nuevo Vallarta resale competes among similar resort units; differentiation requires marina position, renovation, or rental history proof. Name the state and colonia in listings to avoid buyer confusion.

## How does this comparison stack up for Mexico investors?

Some buyers extend comparison to Punta de Mita ultra-luxury, different price planet ($800K+), lower volume STR, owner-use weighted. Nuevo Vallarta is not Punta de Mita; PV Romántica is not Nuevo Vallarta resort core. Keep comparisons within realistic price bands.

## What checklist should run before you sign?

The state line down the middle of this comparison is the first checklist item, not a footnote. Puerto Vallarta closings run through Jalisco, Nuevo Vallarta through Nayarit, and the escritura path, the municipal STR registration and the relevant authorities all differ accordingly. After that the checks are physical: read the HOA bylaws for any rental-pool obligation, and walk the guest path yourself — the Romántica hills and the distance from Nuevo Vallarta parking to the pool are both things that read fine on a map and cost occupancy in practice.

1. Confirm state: Jalisco escritura path vs Nayarit
2. Read HOA bylaws for STR or rental pool obligation
3. Walk guest path: Romántica hills or Nuevo Vallarta parking to pool
4. Model net with rental pool split if applicable
5. Verify municipal STR registration path for correct municipality
6. Compare net to [Los Cabos San José](/areas/san-jose-del-cabo/) if choosing between Pacific markets
7. Document all-in basis with CFDI for ISR exit

## Bottom line

Puerto Vallarta Zona Romántica is the default Banderas Bay pick for walkable STR investors targeting 4–5% net on $280K–450K 1BR inventory with retiree-market depth. Nuevo Vallarta fits resort-amenity operators who accept car-oriented family tourism and verify independent STR or favorable rental pool economics on $350K–550K Nayarit product. Same bay on the map, different spreadsheets.
