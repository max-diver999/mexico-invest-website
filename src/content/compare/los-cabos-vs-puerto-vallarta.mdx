## Yield comparison (1br indicative)

PV Zona Romántica achieves 4.5% net at $340K while San José del Cabo centro reaches 4.2% at $420K, demonstrating Puerto Vallarta's yield advantage on lower entry basis, though Cabo Corridor branded product compresses net below 3.5% due to premium HOA and luxury positioning costs.

PV Zona Romántica and San José del Cabo centro are the two rows that matter, and they are closer than the coasts' reputations suggest. Zona Romántica nets 4.5% on $340,000, returning about $15,300; San José nets 4.2% on $420,000, returning roughly $17,640. The Cabos unit earns $2,340 more on $80,000 more capital, a 2.9% return on the increment. Corridor branded product at 3.2% on $550,000 trails both markets and returns $17,600 — the same income as San José for $130,000 more.

## Entry price and budget tiers

Puerto Vallarta offers accessible entry near $280K in Versalles zones while Los Cabos maintains $350K+ floors in San José centro, reflecting PV's broader price spectrum versus Cabos premium positioning, though both markets add 5–10% closing costs on all tiers.

Range, not the midpoint, is where the two markets genuinely differ. Puerto Vallarta spans $280,000 in Versalles to $700,000 in Conchas Chinas, so a buyer can enter, scale or trade within one market and one manager relationship. Los Cabos starts near $350,000 and runs past $900,000 on the Corridor, which removes the entry tier entirely. For a first Mexican purchase under $350,000, Puerto Vallarta is the only one of the two with inventory, and closing costs of 5% to 10% — roughly $14,000 to $28,000 on a $280,000 Versalles unit — apply identically in both.

| Tier | Los Cabos | Puerto Vallarta |
|---|---:|---:|
| Value 1BR | Limited ~$350K floor | ~$280K Versalles |
| Core investor 1BR | $350K–500K SJC | $300K–420K Romántica |
| Luxury | $550K–900K+ Corridor | $450K–700K Conchas Chinas |
| Closing | 5–10% both | 5–10% both |

## Puerto Vallarta advantages

Puerto Vallarta's Zona Romántica provides walkable STR operations with established retiree infrastructure and net yield advantages, often beating Cabo Corridor performance at lower price points while offering culinary tourism depth and Nuevo Vallarta resort-family alternatives on shared PVR airport access.

Walkable STR core: Zona Romántica cobblestones, guest reviews cite dining walk without car.

Retiree infrastructure: Healthcare, expat services, longer-stay owner ecosystem.

Net yield edge: Romántica often beats Cabo Corridor on net at lower price point.

Culinary tourism depth: Restaurant density supports shoulder-season ADR.

Nuevo Vallarta option: Resort-family thesis on same PVR airport without leaving bay market.

## Walkability and guest experience

Puerto Vallarta's full Zona Romántica walkability outperforms Los Cabos where car dependency affects most zones except San José centro, creating guest logistics advantages for PV STR operators who prioritize review scores and reduced transportation friction over desert luxury branding.

| Element | Los Cabos | Puerto Vallarta |
|---|---|---|
| Romántica-style old town | San José partial | Full Zona Romántica |
| Car required | Often yes | Romántica no |
| Hillside stairs | Some Pedregal | Conchas Chinas |
| Marina tourism | CSL + Corridor | Marina + Nuevo Vallarta |
| Nightlife STR reviews | CSL party risk | Romántica mixed |

STR operators prioritising guest walk scores default PV Romántica unless targeting Cabos luxury ADR.

## Retiree inflow and what it does to a rental thesis

Puerto Vallarta has the deeper retiree market and that cuts both ways: it supports year-round demand and it fills buildings with owner-occupiers who vote against nightly rentals. Los Cabos skews to affluent second-home buyers instead — fewer anti-rental votes, heavier luxury HOA stacks. For a retiree not renting, PV usually wins; for pure short-term rental, Romántica and San José are close on net and PV is cheaper to enter.

Puerto Vallarta absorbs retiree inflows, buildings may restrict STR as owner mix shifts. Verify regime de condominio.

Los Cabos skews affluent second-home, fewer retiree-budget towers but higher HOA luxury stacks.

Retiree not renting: PV often wins. Pure STR: Romántica vs San José, tie on net, PV cheaper entry.

## Flight connectivity

Both airports serve the same core origins — Southern California, the Bay Area, Texas — so connectivity rarely decides this comparison at the national level. It decides it at yours: check your own origin city and season, because a direct route in winter and a connection in September changes the guest mix and the shoulder-month occupancy more than any market-level statistic.

SJD and PVR airports serve overlapping US west-coast and Texas markets with seasonal route variations, requiring origin-specific research since both hubs compete for Southern California and Bay Area traffic while Midwest coverage varies annually between destinations.

| Origin bias | Cabos (SJD) | PV (PVR) |
|---|---|---|
| Southern California | Strong | Strong |
| Bay Area | Strong | Strong |
| Pacific Northwest | Moderate | Moderate |
| Texas | Strong | Strong |
| Midwest | Seasonal | Seasonal |
| Canada | Winter seasonal | Winter seasonal |

Check your city's direct route map before assuming either hub.

## What risks should buyers plan for before they commit?

Los Cabos faces high HOA escalation in Corridor zones and desert water stress while Puerto Vallarta encounters retiree HOA politics affecting STR allowances and hillside utility pressure, creating different execution risks where Cabos emphasizes cost management and PV requires HOA relationship maintenance.

| Risk | Los Cabos | Puerto Vallarta |

## Buyer profile matrix

The two markets sort buyers cleanly. Anyone optimising for walkable short-term rental yield, retiree services or a lower entry ticket lands in Puerto Vallarta. Anyone buying desert-coastal luxury, marina and fishing demand, or branded product at $600,000-plus lands in Los Cabos. Very few buyers are genuinely torn once they say which of those they are.

Walkable STR maximizers and first-time Pacific buyers benefit from PV Romántica's operational advantages, while California desert luxury seekers and marina fishing ADR strategies align with Cabos positioning, creating clear buyer fit distinctions beyond price point considerations.

## Legal stack

The ownership mechanics are identical: both markets sit in the restricted zone, both use a fideicomiso, both charge ISR at sale, and both require the same ejido screening. The one operational difference is jurisdictional — Banderas Bay crosses from Jalisco into Nayarit, so a Puerto Vallarta buyer has to match the attorney to the state on the deed.

Identical national frame, fideicomiso in restricted zone, ISR on sale, ejido avoidance.

PV spans Jalisco and Nayarit municipalities, match attorney to deed state.

## Decision framework

The decision comes down to what you want the property to be. Puerto Vallarta if it is a walkable rental business with a 4–5% net target and retiree-market depth behind it; Los Cabos if it is a premium second home where appreciation and desert-coastal branding matter more than the yield line.

Choose Puerto Vallarta if:

- Walkable Romántica STR thesis
- Retiree services matter
- Lower entry with 4–5% net target
- Culinary guest profile
- PVR flight map fits origin

Choose Los Cabos if:

- Desert-coastal luxury brand
- San José walkable within Cabos
- Owner-use weeks dominate
- Baja scarcity premium acceptable
- SJD flight map fits origin

## Property tax and carrying cost nuance

Predial relatively low both markets, HOA dominates carrying cost difference. Predial is negligible in both — a few hundred dollars a year — so the carrying-cost comparison is really an HOA comparison. Corridor Cabos luxury stacks run $700–1,100 a month against $200–600 in walkable PV, and over a seven-year hold that gap exceeds the difference in purchase price on many pairings.

Corridor Cabos HOA can double Romántica HOA, same gross yield produces very different net.

Stress-test: reduce gross 6% to net using each coast's typical HOA band before choosing.

## Same-country legal exit

ISR on sale applies both, documented basis equally critical. Both markets withhold ISR at the notario on the same two methods — 25% of gross price or 35% of documented gain — so the CFDI file is worth exactly as much on either coast, and it is the one piece of exit preparation that cannot be created retroactively.

Some investors sell PV after 10-year hold and redeploy to Playa for yield, legal mechanics identical, operational reset required.

## Luxury ADR ceiling comparison

Corridor Cabos ADR ceiling exceeds Romántica on ultra-luxury, median investor 1BR ADR gap smaller than marketing suggests. That is the trap in comparing these markets on headline rates: the Corridor ceiling is genuinely higher, but the ceiling belongs to $2M-plus villas rather than to the investor one-bedroom most readers are actually pricing. On comparable stock the ADR gap narrows to something closer to 10–15%.

Run platform comp for your exact bedroom count before assuming Cabos ADR premium justifies lower net.
