## Sample unit economics: centro 1br

Centro 1BR at $310K purchase with 68% occupancy and $130 ADR yields 4.4% net after 25% management and $300 monthly HOA costs, demonstrating capital efficiency advantages over Playacar's higher entry with premium positioning but lower percentage returns.

Occupancy is doing all the work in this model and it deserves scepticism. At 70% and a $130 ADR the unit grosses $33,000 and nets 6.0% on the $326,000 all-in; at 65% gross falls to about $30,700 while management drops only $575 and the $300 HOA, predial and trust fees do not move at all, so net lands near 4.4%. Five points of occupancy costs 160 basis points. Playa Centro's real annual figure sits at 68% to 78% in stabilised listings with a September trough near 50% to 62%, so underwrite the conservative row and treat the aggressive one as upside.

Assume $310K purchase, $16K closing (all-in $326K):

| Line | Annual USD |
|---|---:|
| Gross rent (70% occ, $130 ADR) | ~$33,000 |
| Management 25% | −$8,250 |
| HOA $300/mo | −$3,600 |
| Taxes, trust, misc | −$1,500 |
| NOI | ~$19,650 |
| Net yield | ~6.0% on all-in, aggressive |

Conservative 65% occupancy drops net toward 4.4%, the base case in our colonia tables.

## 2026 market tone

Playa has moved from a seller's market to a selective one, and the split runs by building rather than by district. After the post-2022 run, resale now shows: Prime walkable Centro and Gonzalo Guerrero stock with a rental history still trades close to asking; generic towers without one carry real negotiating room. The market-wide statistic averages those two and describes neither.

- Longer DOM on generic towers
- Motivated seller discounts on secondary units
- Developer new phases still clearing to lifestyle buyers

You can negotiate on resale; do not assume universal 20% haircuts.

## What checklist should run before you sign?

Essential STR building verification includes written HOA STR permission, under 30 competing units, HOA delinquency below 10%, and parking escritura inclusion to avoid operational surprises and yield compression from oversaturated towers or management conflicts post-purchase.

Competing-unit count is the check most buyers skip and the one that predicts revenue best. A building where 40 near-identical 1BRs list on the same platform gives no owner any pricing power, because the only lever anyone holds is rate and the whole cluster races it downward together. Under 30 competing units is a workable threshold in Playa. Pair it with HOA delinquency below 10% and written short-term-rental permission in the condominium regime, and you have answered the three questions that separate a 4.4% net from a 3% one in the same colonia.

- [ ] STR allowed in bylaws (written)
- [ ] Under 30 competing identical STR units
- [ ] HOA delinquency under 10%
- [ ] No pending special assessment votes
- [ ] Parking escritura included
- [ ] Property manager quote in hand pre-close

## Property manager selection in Playa

Playa's competitive STR management market requires screening for building saturation, permit compliance inclusion, owner reporting frequency, and termination clause flexibility since manager selection impacts NOI more than colonia choice through operational execution and fee structure variations across dozens of operators.

Playa has dozens of STR operators, quality varies:

| Question for manager | Why |
|---|---|
| How many units in my building? | Conflict / saturation |
| Permit compliance included? | Fines kill NOI |
| Owner reporting frequency? | Remote oversight |
| Cleaning fee structure? | Hidden cost |
| Termination clause? | Exit if underperform |

Interview two managers before offer, not after closing. Manager quote should enter your net yield spreadsheet pre-offer.

The rail link changes who can reach Playa cheaply, which matters more for shoulder-season occupancy than for peak. Tren Maya's Playa del Carmen stop improves overland connectivity to Cancún airport corridor and inland Yucatán sites. Effects for investors:

Infrastructure supports demand ceiling, does not fix HOA-banned STR building.

## 2026 negotiation playbook

Negotiating room in Playa is not distributed evenly; it sits with owners whose carrying costs have outrun their rental income. Four signals identify them before you make an offer: Two of these appearing together is typically worth 10–15% off asking; one on its own is worth asking the question. None of them requires an agent to tell you, all four are visible from the listing history and the building itself.

- DOM over 90 days in generic tower
- Price reductions on listing history
- Seller carrying two Playa units, portfolio trim
- HOA special assessment recently paid by seller

Counter-offer levers beyond price:

- Seller pays portion of fideicomiso modification
- Furniture inclusion
- Extended closing for your DD comfort
- Developer-paid closing credits on new phases (less common)

## Hurricane and seasonality planning

Six months of the year carry both weather risk and the weakest occupancy, and an annual average yield hides all of it. Atlantic hurricane season, June to November, softens summer occupancy. Operators respond with September is the floor at 50–62% occupancy in Playa Centro against 78–88% in February, on ADRs roughly a third lower.:

- Monthly discounts July–September
- Longer-stay digital nomad pricing
- Maintenance windows in low season

Budget 2–3 months of below-average occupancy in underwriting, not failure, seasonality.

## Playacar deep dive: when premium makes sense

Playacar costs more per square metre than Centro and earns less per night, which makes it wrong for a pure yield brief and right for a specific one. It suits: Gated security, golf and beach club access are what the premium buys, and they matter to a family or an owner-user in a way they do not to a yield model.

- Family STR (longer stays, lower turnover)
- Golf/lifestyle buyers
- Guests avoiding Centro nightlife noise

Trade-offs:

- Higher HOA ($400–900+)
- Car dependency for many guests
- Lower net yield vs Centro on same gross

Not wrong, different guest profile. Underwrite separately.

## North Playa: opportunity with a due-diligence tax

North of Centro is where the new supply is, which means better buildings at lower prices and a longer list of things to verify before you commit. Newer towers there offer: The diligence tax is real: rental permission written into a young regime, developer delivery record, and how far the walk to the beach actually is once the tower is finished.

- Modern amenities
- Lower entry than beachwalk
- Variable HOA health

DD tax: thinner resale history, less STR track record, developer concentration risk. First-time buyers often safer in Centro with proven operating data.

## Exit liquidity: selling your Playa unit

Playa is the most liquid resale market in the corridor, which is a large part of why it justifies its premium over Tulum. What that liquidity rests on: A broad foreign buyer pool that already knows the market, a broker network that functions like an MLS, and enough cash buyers that a financing fall-through does not kill a deal; none of which Tulum can currently match.

- AMPI broker network deep
- Foreign buyer pool continuous
- USD pricing common

Typical resale timeline 60–120 days priced correctly in Centro, versus 90+ in challenged Tulum pockets.

Prepare exit from day one: CFDI basis, HOA docs organised, STR performance records for buyer.

## Tax and basis discipline for Playa investors

Playa's active resale market means many buyers sell within 5–7 years. ISR at sale hits hard without CFDI basis. Without documented invoices the withholding is 25% of the gross sale price rather than 35% of the actual gain, which on a property that appreciated modestly is often the larger number. Every closing cost and capital improvement belongs in the file from day one.

At purchase:

- Invoice every closing component
- Track capital improvements with receipts
- File US depreciation schedule with CPA if applicable

## Insurance and liability for STR operators

A guest injury in a unit you let nightly is a different exposure from an owner-occupied condo, and the HOA master policy will not cover it. Playa STR owners carry: Liability sized for paying guests, contents cover, and a loss-of-rents rider for the weeks a repair takes the unit offline, the last of which is usually the largest uninsured loss after a storm.

- Liability coverage per guest injury
- Property damage from guests
- Business interruption optional

HOA master policy ≠ your unit interior, confirm with broker.

---

## Noise, events, and HOA conflict

The walkability that drives Centro's nightly rate is the same thing that generates the complaints, and HOA fines for guest noise land on the owner rather than the guest. Centro proximity to nightlife, guest noise complaints trigger HOA fines.

Mitigations: noise monitors, quiet hours in listing, guest screening.

One fine cycle can prompt anti-STR owner faction, monitor minutes.
