## What "conservative" means in Mexico coastal real estate

Conservative does not mean "fear Mexico." It means accepting single-digit net returns in exchange for title clarity, operator depth, and exit buyers who exist when you sell. You sacrifice Tulum upside stories and $150K sticker fantasies for predictable operations.

| Conservative priority | Speculative priority |
|---|---|
| Documented title + fideicomiso | Launch pricing |
| Net yield after HOA | Gross broker deck |
| Resale comparables | Brand narrative |
| 7+ year hold | 24-month flip |
| Cash or low leverage | Maximum leverage |

Robert (67) and Linda (64) sold a Calgary rental. Goals: capital preservation, 4% net if achievable, six weeks personal use winter. Risk tolerance: low. They will not manage guests. Six weeks of winter personal use removes roughly 12% of gross from the peak-rate months, so their 4% target has to survive that deduction rather than be measured before it.

Underwriting:

|---|---:|

| Conservative 62% occ | Net ~4.1% |

Outcome: Year-one net 4.0% after September hurricane softness. They rebooked personally without killing annual model.

Lesson: Conservative does not mean "boring building", it means proven building.

## Why Playa beats other markets for conservative thesis

Playa del Carmen combines foreign-buyer depth, walkable STR grids, and net yields that typically exceed Tulum Region 15 on risk-adjusted terms. National price growth in Quintana Roo was strong in 2025, but conservative buyers underwrite income + liquidity, not state-level appreciation headlines.

Predictability, not the headline rate, is what makes Playa the conservative choice. Net yields across its prime colonias cluster between 4.3% and 4.5%, a spread of 20 basis points, while Tulum ranges from 2.6% to 3.4% and Los Cabos from 3.0% to 4.2%. Resale runs 60 to 90 days against 70 to 100 in Cancún and 9 to 18 months for Cabos luxury. A conservative buyer is paying for a narrow distribution of outcomes and a liquid exit, and Playa is the only Mexican coastal market that offers both.

| Cancún hotel zone | Medium | Older stock, HOA variance |

## The conservative yield formula

A conservative net yield is what survives after management, cleaning, HOA, trust fees, insurance and a real vacancy allowance are taken out, divided by everything you actually spent, purchase, closing and furnishing. That last part matters as much as the deductions: modelling on the purchase price alone overstates yield by roughly a tenth on a $300,000 ticket.

All-in basis is the input conservative buyers most often get wrong, and the error flatters the result. On a $300,000 Playa purchase, closing at an 8% planning rate adds $24,000 and furnishing a short-term-rental unit adds $15,000 to $30,000, so the true denominator is $339,000 to $354,000 rather than $300,000. A property netting $13,200 shows 4.4% on the purchase price and 3.7% to 3.9% on what you actually spent. Use the second number, because it is the one your capital experiences.

Never buy on gross. Model:

```
Net = (Gross rent − Management − Cleaning − HOA − Trust − Insurance − Vacancy allowance) / All-in basis
```

All-in includes 5–10% closing, use 8% planning rate on $300K (= $24K).

| Input | Conservative assumption |
|---|---|
| Occupancy | 62–66% year one |
| ADR | Colonia median, not peak week |
| Management | 25% gross STR |
| HOA | Use actual + 10% buffer |
| Closing | 8% on $250K–350K tickets |

## Sample conservative deal: Centro 1BR

A $310,000 Centro one-bedroom lands at $348,000 all-in and returns about 4.8% net at 65% occupancy and a $125 ADR, and 4.2% if occupancy slips to 62% and the rate to $118. That gap between the two lines is the whole conservative argument: underwrite the second number and treat the first as upside.

Purchase $310K · Closing $24K · Furnish $14K · All-in $348K

| Line | USD |
|---|---:|
| Gross @ 65% occ, $125 ADR | $29,600 |
| Management 25% | −$7,400 |
| HOA $320/mo | −$3,840 |
| Trust, predial, insurance | −$1,600 |
| NOI | $16,760 |
| Net yield | 4.8% on all-in, still aggressive |
| @ 62% occ, $118 ADR | ~4.2% net |

Reject if seller math requires 72% occupancy at $140 ADR with $200 HOA.

## HOA: conservative investor's hidden risk

The HOA is the one line item that can turn a 4.5% net into a loss without the property changing at all, and it does it in two ways: a special assessment for a roof, lift or facade, or an assembly vote banning nightly rental outright. Both are visible in advance, in the last 24 months of financials and minutes, and neither appears in a listing.

HOA kills conservative returns when:

- Special assessments pending (roof, elevator, facade)
- Delinquency over 10%
- STR banned in regime de condominio
- Reserve fund underfunded

Required documents:

| Document | Why |
|---|---|
| 24-month financials | Assessment pattern |
| Assembly minutes | STR ban votes |
| Delinquency report | Collectibility |
| Insurance certificate | Hurricane coverage |

## Why conservative investors skip pre-construction

Pre-construction asks a conservative buyer to accept every unknown at once, delivery date, final HOA, permit status, market conditions three years out, in exchange for a discount that resale negotiation often matches anyway. Resale gives you 24 months of HOA history, a building you can walk, and rent starting in weeks rather than in 2028.

Pre-construction offers launch pricing but introduces delivery risk, permit risk, HOA unknowns, and delayed cash flow. Conservative buyers need immediate operability and historical HOA, resale wins.

| Factor | Resale | Pre-con |
|---|---|---|
| HOA history | Known | Unknown |
| STR start | Weeks | 18–36 months |
| Negotiation | DOM-based | Developer schedule |
| Risk | Title + building | Developer + market |

## Conservative financing stance

Cross-border mortgage rates of 9–14% sit above the net yield the property produces, which is why the conservative default in Mexico is cash. If you do borrow, the discipline is leverage low enough that a soft season cannot force a sale: 50–60% LTV, a rate stress test of +200 bps, and a year of payments held in dollars.

Cash purchase simplifies conservative underwriting. If financing:

- LTV 50–60% max (not 70% aggressive)
- Rate stress test +200 bps
- Maintain 12 months payment reserve in USD

## Insurance and catastrophe reserve

A hurricane claim costs a Quintana Roo owner twice: the deductible, typically $3,000–5,000, and the weeks of lost bookings while repairs run. Insurance covers the first and nothing covers the second, so the conservative position is a funded reserve alongside the policy, plus a check that the HOA carries its own building cover.

Quintana Roo faces hurricane season June–November. Conservative owners:

- Wind/flood coverage via MX or US carrier
- $3K–5K deductible reserve
- Generator/water outage plan in HOA review

## Tax conservatism: document everything

Conservative exit strategy requires CFDI at purchase and for capital improvements. Missing basis inflates ISR on sale, 25% gross method or 35% on gain without deductions. On a property that appreciated modestly, 25% of the gross sale price exceeds 35% of the actual gain, which means the CFDI file is worth more than most of the operating decisions across the whole hold.

## How does this comparison stack up for Mexico investors?

Entry-tier product at $150,000 to $200,000 looks like the conservative choice and generally is not, which is worth understanding before reading the table below. Two things dominate the risk-return at that ticket. Closing costs run the same 5-10% as on a larger purchase but consume a larger share of a smaller budget, so a $175K unit can carry $17,500 of unrecoverable acquisition cost against a much thinner equity base. And resale depth at that price point is genuinely poor in Playa, the buyer pool is smaller and slower than the listing volume suggests. ## 7-year conservative hold model (Playa Centro)

Seven years is the shortest hold that amortises 8% of closing costs and still absorbs one bad tourism year without forcing the sale. The model below is deliberately unexciting: stabilise in year one, expect a special assessment somewhere around year three on older stock, and reassess the short-term rental thesis at year six when regulation has had time to move.

| Year | Expected event |
|---|---|
| 1 | Stabilize STR, true net visible |
| 2 | HOA ordinary course |
| 3 | Possible special assessment (aging towers) |
| 4–5 | Steady net + modest appreciation |
| 6 | Reassess STR vs LTR regulatory shift |
| 7 | Exit window, DOM depends on building |

Rule: If net falls under 3.5% for two years and resale comps soft, reallocate, conservatism includes exit discipline.

## What checklist should run before you sign?

Ten checks, and the first three are disqualifying rather than advisory: a net yield below 3.8% at conservative occupancy, unwritten STR permission, or a building already saturated with identical units each end the conversation on their own. The remaining seven decide how well the deal runs, not whether it works.

- [ ] Net yield over 3.8% at conservative occupancy
- [ ] HOA STR allowed, written
- [ ] under 25 identical STR units in building
- [ ] Manager with same-colonia references
- [ ] All-in model includes 8% closing
- [ ] 6–12 month reserve funded
- [ ] CFDI path confirmed with notario
- [ ] Independent attorney engaged
- [ ] No ejido boundary within survey tolerance
- [ ] Personal use weeks fit HOA rental rules

## Municipal STR registration: conservative compliance

Registering before the first guest costs a few weeks; registering after costs a fine, a possible platform delisting, and standing in any HOA dispute. Quintana Roo tightened enforcement through 2024–2026, and the conservative sequence is fixed: HOA permission letter, municipal registration, lodging-tax enrolment, then listing.

Playa del Carmen and broader Quintana Roo tightened STR registration 2024–2026. Conservative investors register before marketing, not after first guest.

| Step | Conservative action |
|---|---|
| HOA STR letter | File copy |
| Municipal registration | Manager files or owner |
| Lodging tax enrollment | SAT compliance |
| Insurance rider | STR endorsement |

Non-compliance risk: fines, platform delisting, HOA litigation, any one erases a year of net.

## Inflation and fee drift: 10-year conservative mode

Fee drift, not vacancy, is what quietly ends most long holds: a 4.2% net at $300 HOA becomes 3.6% at $390 with no change in rent. Over ten years a 30% cumulative rise in HOA and trust fees is the base case, not the stress case, so model the property at the fees it will have in year eight rather than the ones on today's statement.

HOA and trust fees drift upward. Conservative 10-year planning:

| Year | HOA assumption | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Actual | Baseline |
| 4–6 | +15% cumulative | Reserve check |
| 7–10 | +30% cumulative | Reassess STR vs LTR |

A 4.2% net today at $300 HOA becomes 3.6% at $390 HOA without rent growth, model both.

## Conservative Playa DD minimums

Four documents separate a conservative Playa purchase from an optimistic one, and all four are refusable; if a seller or developer will not produce them, that is the answer. Two years of HOA financials, written STR permission, independent rental comparables, and a hurricane insurance quote in writing.

Conservative Playa investors should require two years HOA financials, written STR permission, and independent rental comps, not developer pro formas.
