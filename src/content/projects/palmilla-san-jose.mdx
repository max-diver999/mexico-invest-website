## Palmilla bay: the swimmable cove advantage

Los Cabos' Corridor beaches face open Pacific swells that make many beaches surfable but not family-swimmable. Palmilla Bay is a protected crescent where calm water allows year-round swimming, snorkeling, and paddleboarding. For luxury villa rental marketing, swimmable beachfront in Los Cabos commands 20–35% ADR premium over surf-exposed beachfront.

| Beach type | Water condition | Family appeal | ADR premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pacific-exposed surf beach | Rough, advisory signs | Limited | Base |
| Sea of Cortez beach | Calm, warm | High | +10–15% |
| Protected cove (Palmilla) | Calm Pacific, swimmable | Very high | +20–35% |

Palmilla Bay's cove geometry creates this calm-water condition on the Pacific side, an extremely rare combination of Pacific location and protected swimming conditions. For families with children booking luxury villas, this distinction is decisive.

## Rental economics in the Palmilla zone

Palmilla Bay's combination of protected beach, historical prestige, and One&Only resort adjacency supports ADR at the top of the Los Cabos rental market. Read these as gross figures on a low booked-night base, 55 to 80 nights a year, not 250. Luxury villa rental in Los Cabos is a peak-season business concentrated between November and April, and the gross yield range collapses by roughly half once the 25–35% villa management fee, staffing, pool and landscape maintenance, insurance and predial come out. Underwrite the net and treat the gross as marketing.

| Villa type | Nightly rate range | Annual booked nights | Indicative ticket | Gross on those inputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry corridor residence (3BR) | $1,500–$2,500 | 55–70 | $720K | 11–24% |
| Bay-access villa (4BR, pool) | $2,500–$4,000 | 60–80 | $1.1M | 14–29% |
| Oceanfront villa (5BR) | $3,500–$6,000 | 70–90 | $1.8M | 14–30% |

The gross column is simply what each row's own rate and night count produce, and the spread inside every row is the point. The top of each range assumes peak rack rate on every booked night, which no villa achieves across a season; the bottom assumes the low rate throughout. Real performance sits between them and closer to the bottom, because the weeks that fill easiest are the ones that discount. Treat the high end as the operator's case and underwrite on the low end.

After 30–35% management fee, HOA ($1,500–$2,800/month for luxury properties), utilities, and maintenance reserves, net yields land approximately 4–6% across the range. High gross yields at luxury price points compress to moderate net yields after the full cost stack.

At $720K on conservative assumptions, 55 booked nights at a $1,800 blended rate, the arithmetic runs: $99,000 gross, $69,300 after a 30% management fee, then HOA at $1,500 to $2,800 a month takes $18,000 to $33,600, and utilities, maintenance reserve, predial, insurance and the trust fee take roughly $10,000 more. Net lands between about $25,700 and $41,300, or **3.6% to 5.7% on the purchase price** and roughly 3.3% to 5.3% on an all-in basis. That range is consistent with the 4–6% stated above and with Los Cabos generally; any figure above it on this product is assuming either more nights or a higher rate than the row supports.

## Due diligence: Palmilla-specific considerations

Palmilla corridor properties carry specific diligence requirements beyond standard pre-construction checks, reflecting the complex historical land ownership, resort adjacency, and multi-phase masterplan governance. Palmilla's land history is older and more layered than most Los Cabos developments, which is why beach-access easements and shared-amenity rights have to be read in the escritura rather than taken from a sales brochure. Amenity access at an adjacent resort is contractual and can be revoked or repriced; if it is part of why you are buying, it belongs in the deed.

| Diligence item | Palmilla-specific requirement |
|---|---|
| Beach access easement | Verify in escritura, not just marketing |
| One&Only adjacency rights | Confirm what amenities are shared vs. resort-exclusive |
| HOA structure | Multi-phase masterplan with complex governance |
| Water infrastructure | Private system, verify capacity and reliability |
| Environmental permits | Corridor construction requires SEMARNAT review |
| Masterplan phase | Where in development sequence, completion risk |

## Palmilla vs. Pedregal: which fits your thesis?

| Attribute | Palmilla San José | Pedregal Cabo |
|---|---|---|
| Beach | Palmilla Bay, swimmable | No direct beach (5–10 min drive) |
| Views | Bay, sea, historic cove | Panoramic Pacific, Los Arcos |
| Address heritage | 70-year luxury legacy | Hillside premium, newer |
| Entry | $720K | $890K |
| Appreciation driver | Address + bay scarcity | Hillside supply constraint |
| Rental profile | Beach-access villa premium | View villa premium |

These two addresses are the Los Cabos market's oldest rivalry and neither is superior; they serve genuinely different theses, and the deciding factor is usually one line in the table below rather than the balance of all of them. Palmilla has a swimmable bay, which on this coast is scarce enough to be a structural advantage: most Cabo beaches are not safe for swimming, and a family guest base cares about that more than about any amenity. It also carries seventy years of luxury heritage. Pedregal trades the beach for hillside privacy and panoramic Pacific views, with a five-to-ten-minute drive to the water.

Palmilla suits: high-net-worth US, Canadian, and European buyers seeking Los Cabos' most prestigious address with stable long-term appreciation, families wanting a protected beach cove for multi-generational use, and portfolio investors anchoring in supply-constrained Cabo Corridor real estate.
