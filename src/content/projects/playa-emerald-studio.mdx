Everything unusual about this purchase comes from one arithmetic fact: most of what it costs to buy property in Mexico does not scale with the price. A trust costs $2,500 to $4,000 whether the unit is $98,000 or $980,000. An attorney's review of a purchase contract takes the same hours either way. On a half-million-dollar condo those lines disappear into a 5–7% closing; here they are 8–12%, and the trust alone eats roughly 3% of the purchase. The second fact follows from the format: a studio has no interior features to compete on, so its nightly rate is set almost entirely by how far a guest walks. Both facts point the diligence in the same direction, at the closing statement and at the map, rather than at the finish schedule.

## Ownership and closing for foreigners

Foreign buyers acquire via fideicomiso, mandatory for coastal-zone property. At $98K, trust setup represents approximately 3% of purchase, higher proportionally than at $250K price points. Ensure the purchase contract specifies trust formation timeline, beneficiary rights for rental and resale, and penalty terms for delayed delivery.

| Closing on $98K; note which lines move with price | |
|---|---|
| ISAI, 2–3%: scales with price | $1,960–$2,940 |
| Notaría and registry: mostly scales | $1,470–$2,450 |
| Trust setup: flat, whatever you pay | $2,500–$4,000 |
| Attorney: near-flat | $1,200–$2,500 |
| Total, 8–12% here, 5–7% at $500K | ~$8,000–$12,000 |

Buyers who are not resident in Mexico close remotely through a power of attorney; expect 30 to 90 days between contract and a registered trust.

## What checklist should run before you sign?

At $98K this is the lowest entry on the site, and the studio format means location does all the work, a studio five minutes from Quinta Avenida and a studio twenty minutes out are entirely different assets at a similar price. So the site visit is not a formality: walk the actual route to Fifth Avenue and to beach access and time it yourself rather than accepting the brochure's figure. Then the standard Solidaridad file: escritura clear of ejido claims and liens, the construction licence verified at the municipio, capped escrow milestones and a five-year HOA budget with capital reserves.

Before Playa Emerald Studio deposit:

1. Title search: escritura, clear title, no ejido claim, no liens.
2. Permits: licencia de construcción verified at Solidaridad municipio.
3. Site visit: confirm exact walk times to 5th Ave and beach access.
4. Escrow: milestone structure, maximum 10–15% before structure complete.
5. HOA budget: 5-year projection with capital reserve line.
6. STR policy: written authorization in HOA reglamento.
7. ADR comps: studio-specific Playa downtown Airbnb data, not 1BR beach.
8. Attorney contract review: delivery date, penalty clauses, refund terms.

The whole sequence, in the order an attorney would run it, is in [Due Diligence Mexico Real Estate](/guides/due-diligence-mexico-real-estate/).
