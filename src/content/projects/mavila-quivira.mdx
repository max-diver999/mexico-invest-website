Mavila lets buyers enter Quivira's golf-and-Pacific ecosystem without Copala's $610K floor or St. Regis ultra-luxury basis. This review covers pricing, fee economics, yield math, and buyer fit.

Buying the cheapest home in an expensive neighbourhood is an old strategy, and in a master plan it comes with a specific mechanic worth understanding. Quivira's amenity level, golf, beach club, security, landscaping, service, is calibrated to what its ultra-luxury tiers expect and are content to fund. An entry-tier owner receives that same standard and contributes toward it on terms that are largely fixed per unit rather than proportional to value. That works in your favour on the amenity side: for $329,000 you get infrastructure no standalone building at that price could offer. It works against you on the arithmetic, because the monthly figure that a $4M owner treats as noise is, for you, the difference between a 4.2% net and something closer to 3%. Model the dues first and the price second. Budget extra time for the membership transfer and HOA approval on a resale: in a master-planned community those are closing prerequisites rather than formalities, and they routinely add two to four weeks.

## What risks should buyers plan for before they commit?

Mavila risks include building-specific HOA weakness, Quivira fee increases, 1BR oversupply in soft markets, STR restriction changes, and special assessments on shared Quivira infrastructure. Resale buyers face as-is condition risk, inspect thoroughly. One-bedroom inventory is the most exposed of the three in a soft market, because it competes with every other one-bedroom in the plan on a near-identical product.

| Risk | Action |
|---|---|
| Weak HOA building | Avoid, check litigation |
| Fee escalation | 10-year model |
| STR rules | Written confirmation |
| Deferred maintenance | Physical inspection |
| Membership transfer | Verify before deposit |

## Due diligence workflow

Diligence on a Mavila resale runs at two levels: the unit and building, HOA financials, reserve position, assessment history, written rental permission, and the master plan above it, where the community association fee trajectory and reserve adequacy determine your carrying cost for the whole hold. Both need checking before an offer, not after.

Before Mavila at Quivira offer:

1. Identify specific building: Mavila is not monolithic.
2. Pull building-specific resale comps last 12 months.
3. Review HOA financials: reject weak reserves.
4. Confirm Quivira membership tier and transfer cost.
5. Verify STR allowance in writing.
6. Model net yield on your unit: not portfolio average.
7. Inspect physical condition: resale as-is risk.
8. Engage attorney per [Due Diligence Mexico Real Estate](/guides/due-diligence-mexico-real-estate/).
