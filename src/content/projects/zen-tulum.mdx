## STR operations for wellness residences

Wellness STR in Tulum requires more active curation than standard condo STR. Guests booking $220/night wellness residences expect curated arrival experiences, wellness programming schedules, clean-food kitchen stocking, and local cenote tour connections. Management fees for wellness STR typically run 28–35% of gross revenue vs 22–25% for standard product.

| Ops factor | Wellness reality |
|---|---|
| Management fee | 28–35% of gross |
| Guest expectations | Curated experience, not just clean space |
| HOA | $200–400/month (amenity-heavy) |
| Occupancy seasonality | Strong Oct–Apr, softer May–Sep |
| Review sensitivity | Wellness guests review extensively |

Worked on an entry unit rather than the top of the range. A $198,000 purchase becomes roughly $229,000 all-in after 8% closing and $15,000 of furnishing. At a $130 ADR and 62% annual occupancy, Aldea Zama-comparable assumptions rather than beachfront ones, gross revenue is about $29,400. Take out management at 30% ($8,820), HOA at $300 a month ($3,600), utilities ($1,800) and maintenance ($1,500), and net operating income is roughly $13,680: about 6.0% net on all-in cost. Raising the ADR assumption to $200 is what produces the double-digit figures in developer material, and $200 a night is beachfront Tulum pricing rather than jungle-corridor pricing on a $198,000 unit.

Notice which variables move that outcome: management quality, programme execution, and what the neighbours build. None of them is architectural, and none is fixed at purchase. A conservative base case runs 4–5.5% net on all-in cost once occupancy is stressed to 50–55% and the HOA is taken at its delivered rather than its marketed figure. ## Developer diligence: Zen Tulum hábitat

Tulum pre-construction carries specific risks beyond general Mexico off-plan risk. The ejido land issue is the most critical, a significant portion of Tulum jungle interior sits near or on communal ejido land that has generated long-running title disputes, SEMARNAT stop-work orders, and delivery cancellations across multiple Tulum developments.

| Tulum-specific risk | Required action |
|---|---|

| SEMARNAT permits | Environmental clearance in escritura |
| Cenote protection zone | Verify development complies with 50m setback rules |
| HOA wellness obligations | Written governance for amenity maintenance |

## What checklist should run before you sign?

Wellness-positioned product in Tulum tends to sit on the kind of jungle parcel where two environmental rules apply at once, and at $198K both belong ahead of the finish schedule. Commission a professional survey confirming no communal-land overlap within 500 metres, not a developer assurance, a survey. Then establish that the SEMARNAT environmental impact authorisation is physically in the developer's possession before your deposit moves, and that the layout respects the 50-metre cenote protection setback. A cenote discovered after foundations is a redesign, and redesigns are paid for by buyers.

1. Ejido boundary: professional survey confirming title with no communal land overlap within 500m.
2. SEMARNAT: environmental impact authorization in developer's possession before deposit.
3. Cenote setback: development complies with Mexican environmental 50m cenote protection rule.
4. Construction permits: licencia de construcción verified at Tulum/Felipe Carrillo Puerto municipio.
5. Escrow: milestone-based, max 15% before concrete structure complete.
6. Wellness spec: all amenities itemized and legally binding in purchase agreement.
7. HOA pro forma: 5-year projection including wellness programming maintenance.
8. STR authorization: written permission and proposed management agreement before signing.
9. Comps: wellness jungle ADR from Tulum, not beachfront or Region 15 standard condos.
10. Attorney review: default, refund, delay, and spec-downgrade remedies in contract.
