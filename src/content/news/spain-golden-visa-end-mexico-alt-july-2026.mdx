## What changed in Spain

Spain's Golden Visa closed to new applicants in 2025, removing the property-linked residency route that drew much of the foreign buying interest. Mexico never tied residency to a purchase price, so the immigration and investment theses have always been separate here — which is why a buyer arriving from a Spain comparison should re-underwrite on yield and liquidity rather than on status.

| Before | After (verify counsel) |
|---|---|
| €500K+ property → GV path | Real-estate route largely closed 2025 |
| Costa del Sol STR marketing | Municipal licence caps persist |
| Non-resident 24% rent cited | Still applies, independent of GV |

Mexico never tied residency to purchase price, immigration and investment theses stay separate.

## Why US buyers compare Mexico now

The pull is entry price and proximity rather than residency: Mexican coastal stock starts near $200,000 with direct flights from most US hubs, and prime Playa colonias net 4.3% to 4.5% after the full fee stack. Neither country now offers a residency path through a purchase.

| Factor | Mexico RM | Spain coastal |
|---|---|---|
| Flight from US | 2–5 hours | 8–12+ hours |
| STR US guest demand | Very high | Moderate |
| Entry 1BR | $200K–350K | €250K–450K+ |
| Net yield indicative | 4–5% Playa | 3–4% licensed |
| Residency via property | No | Largely closed |

## Due diligence reminder

Mexico fideicomiso is not simpler than Spain, different, not easier. Ejido screening, HOA STR bans, and SAT 2026 withholding apply regardless of Spain headline fatigue. Concretely: the trust costs $2,500 to $4,000 to establish and $500 to $800 a year, the SRE permit inside that is a MXN 21,650 federal duty, and none of it substitutes for the ejido screening or the written rental clause in the régimen de condominio.
