Quick answer: San Pancho is a small Riviera Nayarit town seven kilometres north of Sayulita and about an hour from Puerto Vallarta airport, with a strong community culture and a quieter character than its neighbour. Every Pacific coast town in Mexico has a version of the same conversation about what it used to be. San Pancho is unusual in that it has held on to a good deal of it, largely because Sayulita absorbed the growth first.

That is the market in one sentence, and it is also its risk.

## Zones and indicative prices

The town is small enough to walk across and divides into the flat part near the beach, the hillsides on either side, and the area up the valley behind. Asking levels below were observed on public listings in September 2026.

| Zone | Indicative band | Character | Note |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Town centre, flat | $250,000 to $450,000 | Walkable, restaurants, the plaza | Small lots, some road noise |
| Near the beach | $400,000 to $900,000 | Short walk to sand, mixed stock | Highest demand, tightest supply |
| North hillside | $450,000 to $900,000 | Ocean views, jungle, privacy | Access road quality varies |
| South hillside | $400,000 to $850,000 | Views toward the point | Steeper, some tracks |
| Up the valley | $200,000 to $450,000 | River, jungle, larger plots | Flood path, access in rainy season |
| Lots and land | $120,000 to $400,000 | Buildable parcels | Title chain is the whole question |

The supply constraint is real. San Pancho has a small buildable footprint, hemmed by hillsides and a river valley, and the flat land near the beach is largely built. That underpins prices and it also means the market moves in steps when a few properties trade rather than smoothly.

## The Sayulita comparison, honestly

These two towns are compared constantly and the comparison is usually made badly, on price, when the real difference is what daily life feels like.

| | San Pancho | Sayulita |
|---|---|---|
| Distance apart | 7 km | 7 km |
| Character | Residential, quieter, community led | Commercial, busy, younger |
| High season crowding | Noticeable | Intense |
| Beach | Wide, shore break, currents | Sheltered bay, surf school friendly |
| Rental demand | Steady, longer stays | Higher volume, shorter stays |

A buyer choosing between them should spend a February afternoon in each and decide on that rather than on a spreadsheet, because the numbers will not separate them and the experience will. ## The river, the jungle and the plot

Three physical features shape what is buildable here and what a plot is actually worth.

**The river.** San Pancho has a seasonal river running to the beach through the town. In the dry season it is a feature. In September it is a river. Any plot in the valley or near the mouth needs its relationship to the flood path established from local knowledge and, where possible, from the municipality, not from a dry-season walk.

**The hillsides.** The views come from elevation and the elevation comes with access. Several attractive parcels are reached by unpaved tracks that a normal car manages in April and struggles with after heavy rain. Ask what the last owner drove and what the track looks like in September.

**Ejido history.** The Nayarit coastal strip carries the same agrarian past as most of coastal Mexico. Any parcel whose chain touches ejido needs the dominio pleno conversion documented in the public registry, and any seller who describes it rather than showing it should be treated accordingly. The mechanism is set out in [ejido land risks in Mexico](/guides/ejido-land-risks-mexico/).

**Insider tip:** ask to see the property in the last week of September if you can, or ask three neighbours what that week looks like. The rainy season is the honest test of access, drainage and the river here, and every listing photograph is taken in the dry months.

## The community institutions, and why they hold value

Most Mexican beach towns are described by their beach. San Pancho is more usefully described by what happens away from it, because that is the thing a buyer is actually paying a premium for over an equivalent house further up the coast.

The town has an unusually dense layer of community organisations for its size: an education and arts centre that runs programmes for local children and recycles materials the town would otherwise burn, a circus school, sports facilities, and an established calendar of events that runs through the year rather than only in high season. Much of it traces back to a period of state investment decades ago that left the town with infrastructure out of proportion to its population, and to a subsequent generation of residents who kept it running.

This matters commercially in two ways that a listing never explains. It gives the town a resident population and a year-round economy, so restaurants and services do not shutter in September the way they do in purely seasonal places. And it produces the specific character that buyers cite when they choose here over the busier neighbour, which is what supports prices at a level comparable to Sayulita despite lower rental volume.

The risk attached to that is worth naming too. A town's character is not a legal protection. What preserved San Pancho was largely that Sayulita grew first, and growth pressure on this coast has continued to move north. A buyer paying for the atmosphere should understand they are paying for something that has no guarantee attached, and should be comfortable with the house on its own terms.


## Pros and cons

**Pros.** A genuinely intact town character within an hour of an international airport. Strong community institutions that give the place year-round life rather than a season. A wide beach without Sayulita's density. Supply constrained by geography, which supports values. Established foreign ownership with the closing infrastructure that brings.

**Cons.** Prices comparable to Sayulita without Sayulita's rental volume. A beach with a shore break that does not suit every family. A small market where resale depends on a handful of buyers. Access tracks on the hillsides that need checking in the wet season. The same growth pressure that changed the neighbour is pointed here next.

## Which buyer this suits

**Someone who wants a town rather than a resort.** San Pancho is a real place with a plaza, a school and a community centre, and that is the product.

**A longer-stay owner or part-year resident.** The rental pattern here favours weeks and months over nights, which suits an owner who uses the house themselves and lets around it.

**A buyer priced out of Punta de Mita but wanting the same coast.** The Riviera Nayarit price ladder runs steeply, and this is a rung on it.

It suits a high-volume nightly rental investor least, since Sayulita seven kilometres away simply does that better.
