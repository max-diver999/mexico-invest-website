## Why does Loreto price below the rest of Baja?

Loreto runs $1,600-3,400 USD per square metre in 2026 against $1,800-3,600 in La Paz and $3,500-7,000 in the Cabo Corridor. The discount is not a quality discount: it reflects flight access and town size, and foreign buyers who understand that distinction price the market correctly.

| Factor | Loreto | La Paz | Los Cabos |
|---|---|---|---|
| USD per sqm, typical | $1,600-3,400 | $1,800-3,600 | $3,500-7,000 |
| Population | ~19,000 | ~300,000 | ~350,000 |
| Direct international flights | Limited, variable | Mostly domestic | Extensive |
| Typical days on market | 200+ | 120-200 | 90-150 |
| Net yield band | 2.5-3.5% | 4.5-5.5% | 3-5% |
| Summer letting support | Very thin | Resident base | Thin |

Population explains most of the yield gap, and the mechanism is worth stating plainly rather than left as a size comparison. La Paz can re-let a unit to a hospital administrator or a university lecturer when tourist demand thins in July, converting a dead month into MXN 18,000-24,000 of rent. Loreto has no equivalent pool at 19,000 residents, so a property here earns across roughly 130-150 season nights or it earns nothing, and that single structural difference accounts for most of the 200 basis points separating the two markets. Model it against the [rental yield guide](/guides/mexico-rental-yield-guide/) before comparing headline gross figures.

## What FONATUR built, and what it did not

FONATUR is Mexico's federal tourism development fund, and it designated Loreto an original planned resort destination alongside Cancun, Ixtapa and Huatulco. That designation produced infrastructure well beyond what a town of 19,000 residents would otherwise carry: an international airport, a marina, a golf course and serviced land across the Nopolo and Puerto Escondido zones, all of which foreign buyers inherit at $2,400-3,400 per square metre.

What the master plan did not produce is demand at the scale it was sized for, and that gap has persisted for four decades rather than closing. Cancun absorbed its FONATUR infrastructure within fifteen years and now handles over 30 million air passengers a year; Loreto's airport still runs a handful of routes that expand and contract with individual airline decisions. For a foreign buyer the practical consequence is specific and permanent: the roads, water and power in the planned zones are real and functioning, while the visitor volume that would fill a nightly-rental calendar is not, and no amount of local marketing changes an airline's route economics.

The infrastructure transfers with the escritura and the fideicomiso in the ordinary way; the demand does not transfer at all. Three things follow for anyone underwriting here, and each belongs on a buyer checklist rather than in a brochure:

- **Infrastructure quality in Nopolo and Puerto Escondido genuinely exceeds** what comparable small Mexican towns offer
- **Occupancy assumptions must be built from actual booking records**, never from the master plan's original projections
- **Resale depends on the same limited buyer flow** that limits rental demand, which is why days on market run past 200

## What does property cost in Loreto?

Town-centre homes cost $1,600-2,400 USD per square metre in 2026, while the FONATUR-planned zones south of town range from $2,400 to $3,400. Pricing in this market tracks water, road access and proximity to the marina rather than finish level, and the spread between town and planned zones reflects infrastructure rather than construction quality.

| Zone | USD per sqm | 130 sqm 3BR | Character |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Loreto town centre | $1,800-2,400 | $234K-312K | Walkable, municipal services, historic |
| Nopolo / Loreto Bay | $2,400-3,400 | $312K-442K | Master-planned, golf, beach access |
| Puerto Escondido (marina) | $2,200-3,000 | $286K-390K | Marina, boating community |
| Town periphery | $1,600-2,100 | $208K-273K | Larger lots, verify services |
| Ejido-adjacent fringe | Varies widely | — | Title diligence essential first |

Three factors move a specific property inside these bands, and a buyer checklist should price each separately:

- **A documented dominio pleno conversion** from ejido origin adds 15-25% and removes the market's largest legal risk
- **A confirmed municipal water connection** rather than trucked delivery is worth 8-12% and removes an ongoing $180-350 monthly cost
- **Walking distance to the malecon or marina** supports the nightly rates that peripheral stock cannot achieve

## The marine park: constraint and asset

Bahia de Loreto National Park protects roughly 2,000 square kilometres of islands and waters off this coast, and that designation is simultaneously why visitors come and why coastal supply in this market stays permanently constrained. Sport fishing, diving and island tours form the entire visitor economy, and SEMARNAT review adds 6-18 months to any shoreline permit path.

What the designation means in practice, on both sides of the ledger:

| Effect | Mechanism | What it costs or protects |
|---|---|---|
| Supply constraint | SEMARNAT review, ZOFEMAT concession | Adds 6-18 months to shoreline permits |
| Visitor quality | Protected fishery and reef | Longer stays, annual repeat guests |
| Development ceiling | Park boundary is fixed | Beachfront inventory cannot expand |
| Permit risk | Environmental authorisation required | Stop-work exposure without it |

For a buyer the park creates a two-sided effect worth pricing explicitly. On the supply side, development near the shoreline faces SEMARNAT environmental review and ZOFEMAT federal maritime concession requirements that add roughly 6-18 months to any permit path, which permanently limits how much beachfront inventory can ever exist. On the demand side, the protected fishery sustains a specialist visitor who books longer stays and returns annually, producing a guest profile closer to Cozumel's dive market than to a general beach resort. Neither effect shows up in a listing, and both belong in a hold-period assumption.

## Rental economics: a worked example

A 130 square metre three-bedroom in Nopolo at $2,700 per square metre costs $351,000, and closing at 7% takes the all-in basis to $375,600. Nightly letting in this market typically achieves 130 booked nights at a $185 ADR, which grosses $24,050 a year before operating costs.

| Line | Annual USD |
|---|---:|
| Gross rent, 130 nights at $185 ADR | $24,050 |
| Management at 32% | −$7,696 |
| HOA and common costs | −$3,100 |
| Predial, insurance, trust fee | −$2,400 |
| Utilities, pool, maintenance | −$4,200 |
| Net operating income | $6,654 |

That NOI is 1.8% net on the $375,600 all-in basis at 130 nights, and reaching the 2.5-3.5% band quoted above requires 160-180 nights or an ADR near $240. Both are achievable on well-positioned beachfront stock with an operator who fills shoulder season, and neither is the base case for typical inventory. Where the arithmetic runs against a Riviera Maya comparison:

- Season of roughly 130-150 bookable nights against 250-280 in Playa del Carmen
- Management at 30-35% rather than 25%, because the specialist pool is small
- Trust fee of $500-800 a year, identical to Cabo but on a smaller revenue base
- No resident tenant pool to absorb the summer, unlike La Paz

## Pros and cons for investors

Loreto advantages and constraints derive from one fact: this market has resort infrastructure and small-town demand. That is why the roads and marina work while the nightly calendar does not fill, and why net yields sit at 2.5-3.5% against La Paz at 4.5-5.5%.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| FONATUR infrastructure exceeds town size | Net yield 2.5-3.5%, among the thinnest here |
| Entry 40% below La Paz, 60% below Cabo Corridor | Days on market routinely exceed 200 |
| Marine park permanently constrains coastal supply | Flight access limited and historically variable |
| Specialist fishing and diving visitors return annually | No resident tenant pool for the summer |
| Genuine scarcity; population roughly 19,000 | Management fees 30-35% versus 25% in Riviera Maya |
| International airport for a town of this size | Ejido title history requires full agrarian diligence |

## Who should buy in Loreto?

Three buyer profiles fit this market, and each requires a different entry price: roughly $208,000-273,000 for peripheral town stock bought principally for use, $234,000-312,000 for a walkable town-centre home, and $312,000-442,000 for Nopolo or Loreto Bay property where infrastructure and beach access are strongest.

- Lifestyle owner-user and angler → town centre or Nopolo, $234,000-442,000, rental as cost offset
- Scarcity buyer on a long hold → Nopolo beachfront, $312,000-442,000, 2.5-3.5% net, seven-year minimum
- Marina and boating buyer → Puerto Escondido, $286,000-390,000, slip access verified separately

Each profile holds through a fideicomiso at $500-800 a year. **The yield-focused investor should look at La Paz or Queretaro instead** — nothing in Loreto's demand structure supports a cash-flow thesis, and a model showing 6% net is assuming an occupancy the airline schedule does not deliver.

## What red flags should pause a Loreto purchase?

Six patterns recur in this market and the first two are close to unique to FONATUR destinations. Any one typically costs more to resolve after closing than the MXN 30,000-60,000 a full legal and services review requires beforehand, which is the argument for sequencing the checklist properly.

- Occupancy projections drawn from the FONATUR master plan rather than from booking records
- Nightly assumptions above 180 nights, which the current flight schedule does not support
- Ejido land history on the fringe without documented conversion to dominio pleno
- A water supply that is trucked rather than connected, presented as serviced
- ZOFEMAT concession unverified on any beachfront or beach-adjacent parcel
- Predial arrears, which attach to the property rather than to the seller

## What should you verify before committing?

Eight checks settle a Loreto purchase and the order matters, because each costs more than the last. For foreign buyers the occupancy evidence comes first: this is the one market on this site where the gap between projected and actual bookings routinely exceeds 40%, and no price reduction repairs a revenue assumption built on a master plan.

1. Request two years of actual booking records, not projections or comparables.
2. Check current direct flight schedules to LTO and their history over three seasons.
3. Obtain the complete agrarian exit file where any ejido origin exists.
4. Confirm municipal water connection rather than trucked delivery, in writing.
5. Verify ZOFEMAT concession status on any beach-adjacent parcel.
6. Pull the escritura chain and a certificado de libertad de gravamen.
7. Check predial is current with a constancia de no adeudo.
8. Read the HOA reglamento and reserve fund position in planned-zone developments.

Costs and timings: legal and agrarian review runs MXN 30,000-60,000 over 10-20 business days, the escritura search MXN 4,000-9,000, and a services inspection $600-1,200. Total diligence lands near 1-1.5% of a $351,000 purchase, against a revenue assumption that is the single most commonly overstated figure in this market.
