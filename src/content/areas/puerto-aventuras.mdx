## Why Puerto Aventuras commands a yield premium over Tulum

Puerto Aventuras nets 3.8–4.6% against Region 15's 2.6–3% despite costing more, and the reasons are all structural rather than about the buildings: a master HOA that actually functions, established managers, families booking 8.5-night stays instead of couples booking three, and no wave of identical units competing for the same guest.

Gated master-plan structure is what separates Puerto Aventuras from Tulum's open market, and it works in both directions. A single master HOA at roughly $380 a month funds the marina, security and grounds, so there is no cluster of forty identical units in adjacent buildings competing on rate, which is precisely what takes Region 15 to 2.6% net. The trade is that fee escalation is voted at master-plan level rather than by your building, and an owner has correspondingly little influence over it. Budget the same 5-8% annual escalation you would on any Mexican HOA, from a base of roughly $4,560 a year.

Puerto Aventuras consistently outperforms Tulum's primary investment zones on net yield despite higher prices, for structural reasons:

| Factor | Puerto Aventuras | Region 15 Tulum |
|---|---|---|
| Management complexity | Lower (established HOA, operators) | Higher (variable building quality) |
| HOA governance | Functioning master HOA | Often absent or non-functional |
| Guest safety perception | Highest in Riviera Maya | Mixed (no gating) |
| Average stay | 8.5 nights | 4.2 nights |
| Repeat booking rate | 26% | 14% |
| Family ADR premium | $220–300/night (2BR) | $130–180/night (2BR) |
| Low-season floor | Family year-round demand | Tourist-dependent, deep low-season |

The family segment's longer stays and higher ADR premium more than offset Puerto Aventuras' higher price entry when modelled across a full year.

## Property types and pricing tiers

Family group size drives pricing here more than square metres do, because the master plan's demand is multi-generational. A 1BR inland at $280,000 to $330,000 serves couples and monthly expat tenants; the marina and golf-view tier at $320,000 to $400,000 commands a premium from families who want the walk to the dolphin centre and the promenade; and 3BR townhouses at $430,000 to $580,000 capture whole-group bookings that no condo tier reaches. Match the unit to the group you intend to host.

| Property type | Price range | Best rental strategy |
|---|---|---|
| 1BR condo, inland | $280K–330K | STR tourist/family, monthly expat |
| 1BR marina or golf view | $320K–400K | Family STR, premium weekly |
| 2BR condo with amenities | $380K–500K | Family STR peak focus |
| Townhouse, 3BR | $430K–580K | Large family groups, seasonal |
| Beachfront or waterfront villa | $500K–700K+ | Premium family weekly |

Marina-view properties command a $30K–70K premium over equivalent inland units but achieve higher ADRs that generally justify the markup within 3–5 years of operation.

## Yield modelling for Puerto Aventuras

Two models follow and the realistic answer sits at the lower one. A $420,000 two-bedroom marina condo underwritten at an optimistic 65% occupancy and a $250 ADR shows 6.9% net, which is well above anything the Riviera Maya corridor actually delivers; the conservative case at 55% and $220 lands at 4.4%, in line with Playa's prime colonias. The long average stay here is genuine and does lower turnover cost, but it does not move a Riviera Maya condo into a different yield tier.

Read this column as a forward projection at favourable conditions, not as measured performance. A $250 rate across a 365-night calendar assumes peak-season pricing applied year-round, and the implied gross sits well above the 5.5% to 7% full-year average for the corridor. The conservative scenario that follows is the one to underwrite.

## Seasonal demand patterns

Family demand follows school calendars rather than weather, which flattens the curve: 75–85% in the December-January and March-April holidays, and still 65–75% through the summer when adult-focused Tulum empties. That summer floor is worth more to a leveraged or thinly reserved owner than a higher winter peak.

Family booking rhythm sets this calendar rather than the general Riviera Maya pattern. Puerto Aventuras fills around US and Canadian school holidays, Christmas, spring break, and July to August, which produces a flatter shoulder than Playa's nightlife-driven trade but a sharper dependence on a handful of weeks. Blocking one Christmas week for personal use removes $1,500 to $2,000 of gross at the marina tier, so an owner taking two peak weeks a year should model the yield at roughly 40 basis points below the published figure, which on a $420,000 unit is about $1,700 of foregone income.

Puerto Aventuras' family-focused demand creates more balanced seasonality than tourist-concentrated Tulum:

| Season | Occupancy range | Primary demand |
|---|---|---|
| Peak family (Dec–Jan, Mar–Apr) | 75–85% | US/Canadian holiday families |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | 65–75% | School-vacation families |
| Shoulder (Feb, Nov) | 50–65% | Snowbirds, extended stays |
| Low (May, Sep–Oct) | 40–52% | Mexican domestic, expats |

Low season floor is notably higher than Region 15 because Puerto Aventuras attracts snowbird retirees who take monthly rentals October–April and Mexican domestic family tourists in summer who are unaffected by North American school calendars.

## What checklist should run before you sign?

Five checks, and the first two are specific to this community. The master HOA has raised fees three times in a decade, so verify the current schedule and anything announced; and the Dolphin Discovery concession is a SEMARNAT permit, which means the attraction underwriting your family bookings is subject to regulatory renewal.

- [ ] **Master HOA fee changes**: The community-wide HOA has increased fees three times in the last decade. Verify current fee structure and any announced increases.
- [ ] **Dolphin sanctuary operation**: The facility operates under SEMARNAT concession. Verify it holds current permits and has no active regulatory challenges.
- [ ] **Marina flood risk**: Hurricane season creates marina flooding risk. Review building elevation relative to marina level and insurance coverage requirements.
- [ ] **Golf course property differentiation**: Golf-section properties carry additional maintenance obligations. Confirm specific fees before purchase.
- [ ] **Resale price realistic assessment**: Puerto Aventuras is a mature market. Verify recent comparable sales rather than relying on developer or agent optimistic valuations.
- [ ] **Management company within community**: Verify the management company has operating experience specifically within Puerto Aventuras' HOA rules and gate access procedures.

Puerto Aventuras attracts a specific guest, North American families who want a gated, walkable, marina-based holiday with a dolphin programme and no need for a car, and the buyers who do best here are the ones who underwrite to that guest rather than to the Riviera Maya average. ## Ownership and legal structure

Standard coastal ownership with one extra layer: the fideicomiso is the same $2,500–4,000 as anywhere on the coast, but the master HOA sits above your building's own regime, so two sets of governing documents control what you may do with the property. Read both, the community CC&Rs can restrict rentals that your building permits.

Puerto Aventuras is within the restricted coastal zone requiring fideicomiso for all foreign buyers. The master HOA legal framework adds an additional governance layer:

| Legal element | Puerto Aventuras |
|---|---|
| Fideicomiso requirement | Yes, standard coastal |
| Setup cost | $2,500–4,000 USD |
| Annual trust fee | $500–800 USD |
| Master HOA membership | Mandatory |
| Community CC&Rs | Comprehensive, professionally maintained |
| STR permitting | Community-permitted, HOA governs rules |

## Red flags checklist before you wire funds

Puerto Aventuras is a gated master-planned community with its own security, marina and internal governance, and that structure changes which of these rows bites. Title history here is comparatively clean, the development was master-titled decades ago, so the ejido row is a lower probability than it is in Tulum. The HOA row is the opposite: the community levies at two levels, master and building, and rental permissions vary between regimes inside the same gates. Get the written confirmation for your specific building, not the community.

| Red flag | Why it matters | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Last-minute wire change | Classic BEC fraud pattern | Stop and call notario on verified number |
| No escritura chain review | Title defects surface at sale | Independent notario search before deposit |
| STR promised but not in HOA minutes | Building can block rentals | Written HOA confirmation |
| Ejido-adjacent lot without conversion proof | Foreign ownership risk | Full ejido exit documentation |
| Missing CFDI on improvements | Zero cost basis at ISR sale | Register invoices with SAT early |
| Community and building HOA dues quoted as one figure | Two levies apply, and only one is usually disclosed | Request both schedules and the reserve balance for each |
