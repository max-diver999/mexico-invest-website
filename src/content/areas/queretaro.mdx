Queretaro is the market that most contradicts the assumption that Mexican real estate means a beach. It has no coastline, no tourism economy to speak of, and a tenant base of aerospace engineers and relocating Mexico City professionals — which is precisely why it produces net yields that coastal short-term rentals struggle to match. ## Why does Queretaro sit outside the restricted zone?

Direct ownership by foreign buyers is restricted in Mexico only within 50 km of a coastline and 100 km of an international border, and Queretaro sits roughly 300 km inland from the Gulf and over 400 km from the Pacific. A purchase anywhere in this market therefore requires no fideicomiso, no SRE permit and no annual trust fee, which removes about $2,700-5,000 of setup cost from the closing statement.

| Requirement | Queretaro | Coastal restricted zone |
|---|---|---|
| SRE permit and federal duty | Not required | MXN 21,650 |
| Bank trust establishment | Not required | $500-1,500 |
| Annual trust fee | None | $500-800 per year |
| Title held by | You, directly | Trust bank, you as beneficiary |
| Typical closing time | 30-50 days | 45-90 days |

The saving matters more here than in Mexico City because the entry price is lower. On a $150,000 Juriquilla apartment, avoided trust costs of $2,700-5,000 at closing plus $500-800 annually represent roughly 2-3% of the purchase price up front and about 40 basis points of yield every year — enough to move a net figure from 5.4% to 5.8%.

## What does property cost in Queretaro?

Restored Centro Historico houses cost $1,800-3,000 USD per square metre in 2026, while modern apartments in the northern corridors range from $1,400 to $2,200. Pricing in this market tracks water availability and road access far more than architectural quality, which is the opposite of how Mexico City or Merida price and the first thing foreign buyers get wrong.

| Zone | USD per sqm | 90 sqm 2BR | Character |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Centro Historico | $1,800-3,000 | $162K-270K | UNESCO heritage, restoration rules |
| Juriquilla | $1,600-2,200 | $144K-198K | Established north, lake, universities |
| El Refugio | $1,400-1,900 | $126K-171K | Family suburbs, newest stock |
| Zibata | $1,500-2,100 | $135K-189K | Master-planned, golf, gated |
| Milenio III | $1,300-1,800 | $117K-162K | Value entry, corporate proximity |

Three factors move a specific unit inside these bands, and a buyer checklist should price each separately:

- **A confirmed factibilidad de agua** — the state water feasibility authorisation — adds 10-15% and removes the single largest stall risk
- **Gated access with functioning security** commands 8-12% in a market where safety is the migration driver
- **Centro Historico heritage status** cuts both ways: it constrains supply permanently but restricts renovation, and INAH approval adds 3-6 months to any structural work

## What is actually driving demand here?

Three forces compound in this market, and none of them is tourism. Nearshoring built an aerospace cluster around Queretaro Intercontinental Airport that now anchors Bombardier, Safran and a tier-one supplier base; internal migration from Mexico City accelerated after 2020; and the state records among the lowest homicide rates in the country, which converts temporary postings into permanent residence.

Demand composition explains why this market behaves unlike anywhere else covered on this site. A Playa del Carmen apartment competes for tourists whose numbers move with flight capacity and hurricane season, and its occupancy collapses in September. A Juriquilla apartment houses an aerospace engineer on a three-year contract or a family that left Mexico City permanently, and its occupancy does not have a season at all. That difference shows up as a 35-45% vacancy gap between the two models across a full year, and it is the entire reason Queretaro nets 5-6% on gross yields that look unremarkable next to a coastal headline.

| Demand driver | Tenant produced | Lease length | Cyclicality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aerospace cluster (QRO airport) | Engineers, technicians | 24-36 months | Order-book, 2-4 quarter lag |
| Automotive supply chain | Plant management, staff | 12-24 months | North American vehicle demand |
| CDMX internal migration | Families, remote professionals | 12-24 months | Structural, low cyclicality |
| UNAM and Tec campuses | Academics, graduate students | 10-12 months | Academic calendar |

The corollary is that Queretaro demand tracks industrial investment rather than consumer sentiment. A slowdown in North American automotive or aerospace orders reaches this housing market with a lag of roughly two to four quarters, a real cyclical exposure that a beach market does not share in the same form. The wider industrial picture sits in our [nearshoring guide](/guides/nearshoring-mexico-industrial-real-estate/); the checklist below applies it to a single purchase.

## Rental economics: a worked example

A 90 square metre two-bedroom in Juriquilla at $1,667 per square metre costs $150,000, and closing at 6% takes the all-in basis to $159,000. Long-term letting to a professional tenant in this market typically achieves MXN 16,000 a month, or $10,549 a year at the Banxico reference rate, which is 6.6% gross before operating costs.

| Line | Annual USD |
|---|---:|
| Gross rent, MXN 16,000/month | $10,549 |
| Building maintenance, MXN 1,600/month | −$1,055 |
| Predial (Queretaro) | −$320 |
| Landlord insurance | −$300 |
| Management at 7% | −$738 |
| Vacancy reserve, three weeks | −$608 |
| Repairs reserve at 4% | −$422 |
| Net operating income | $7,106 |

That NOI is 4.5% net on the $159,000 all-in basis at conservative assumptions, and rises toward 5.5-6% where the tenant is corporate-placed and the void falls to two weeks. Where this market beats the coast, line by line:

- No 5% lodging tax, which Quintana Roo charges on accommodation
- Management at 7% rather than 25-35%, because an annual tenancy needs no guest operation
- No furnishing capex of $15,000-30,000 and no year-two refresh
- Void of two to three weeks rather than the 35-45% of nights a nightly calendar leaves empty
- Predial of roughly $320 a year against $550-1,400 in Mexico City

## Water: the constraint that decides which developments survive

Queretaro sits in a semi-arid basin with declining aquifer levels, and water feasibility is the single most important non-title diligence item in this market. The state water operator issues a factibilidad de agua, a formal authorisation confirming a development can be connected, and a project without one can stall regardless of how complete the construction looks.

The factibilidad is issued by the state water operator and recorded against the development rather than the buyer, so it survives a change of owner but not a change of project scope. Aquifer stress is structural rather than cyclical here, which is what distinguishes it from an ordinary infrastructure risk. Queretaro's population has grown faster than almost any Mexican metropolitan area for two decades, largely through in-migration, while its recharge basin has not expanded at all. State authorities have responded by rationing new connection authorisations rather than by expanding supply, which means the factibilidad is now a genuinely scarce asset that transfers with the land. A development holding one trades at a 10-15% premium to an equivalent that does not, and the gap has widened every year since 2020.

Pricing reflects that scarcity directly: a development holding a current factibilidad trades 10-15% above an equivalent without one, adding roughly $15,000-22,000 to a $150,000 apartment. Against that, the review costs MXN 8,000-20,000 and takes about 5 business days through the state operator.

Four checks settle the water question before an offer:

- **Factibilidad de agua in writing**, issued to the specific development and current, not a letter of intent
- **Confirmed connection**, meaning water physically arrives rather than being scheduled
- **Storage capacity on site** — cisterns and pressurisation, which mitigate rationing but not absence
- **The development's history of tandeo**, the rotating supply schedule applied in constrained districts

## Pros and cons for investors

Queretaro's advantages and constraints derive from one fact: this market is industrial and residential rather than touristic. That is why annual tenancies net 5-6% with no lodging tax and no seasonality, and equally why demand tracks aerospace and automotive order books with a two-to-four-quarter lag rather than flight arrivals.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Direct fee-simple title, no trust, no annual fee | No foreign resale pool; exit is to domestic buyers |
| Entry at $1,400-3,000 per sqm, half of Mexico City | Water feasibility can stall a development indefinitely |
| 5-6% net on annual tenancies, strongest band here | Yields depend on industrial employment cycles |
| Among the lowest homicide rates of any state | Almost no short-term rental market to fall back on |
| Predial near $320 a year, no lodging tax | Peso rent against a dollar purchase creates mismatch |
| No hurricane exposure, no seasonality | Centro heritage rules add 3-6 months to structural work |

The exit point deserves weight. Coastal Mexican property resells to foreign buyers who find it through the same channels that found you; Queretaro resells almost entirely to Mexican buyers financed in pesos, which means your exit depends on domestic mortgage conditions and Banxico policy rather than on North American appetite for a beach.

## Who should buy in Queretaro?

Three buyer profiles fit this market, and each requires a different entry price: roughly $117,000-162,000 for a Milenio III or El Refugio yield unit, $144,000-198,000 for a Juriquilla apartment let to corporate tenants, and $250,000-450,000 for a Centro Historico colonial house bought substantially for lifestyle.

- Yield-focused diversifier → El Refugio or Milenio III 2BR, $117,000-171,000, annual tenancy, 5-6% net
- Corporate-tenancy buyer → Juriquilla or Zibata, $135,000-198,000, gated, aerospace tenant base
- Lifestyle and heritage buyer → Centro Historico house, $250,000-450,000, lower yield, INAH constraints

Each profile files one annual ISR return against an RFC and pays predial near $320 a year. **The short-term rental operator should look elsewhere.** Queretaro has a modest business-travel nightly market and nothing resembling the tourism depth of Quintana Roo, so a nightly model here competes for a thin, corporate, weekday-only demand pool.

## What red flags should pause a Queretaro purchase?

Six patterns recur in this market and the first two are close to unique to the Bajio. Any one typically costs more to resolve after closing than the MXN 8,000-20,000 an independent legal and water review requires beforehand, which is the argument for sequencing the checklist properly.

- No factibilidad de agua, or a developer who treats the request as unusual
- A development on tandeo, the rotating water schedule, presented as fully serviced
- Ejido land history on the urban fringe without documented conversion to dominio pleno
- Gated community marketed on security with no functioning guard contract or reserve fund
- Predial arrears of roughly $320 a year, which attach to the property rather than to the seller
- Rent quoted in dollars, signalling a listing aimed at foreigners rather than the real professional tenant market

## What should you verify before committing?

Eight checks settle a Queretaro purchase and the order matters, because each costs more than the last. For foreign buyers the water items come first: a development without a current factibilidad is the single defect on this checklist that no price reduction repairs, and the verification requires about 5 business days and a written response from the state operator.

1. Obtain the factibilidad de agua in writing, issued to this development and current.
2. Confirm water physically arrives, and ask directly about tandeo history.
3. Pull the escritura chain and a certificado de libertad de gravamen from the Registro Publico.
4. Verify no ejido origin on fringe developments, or documented dominio pleno conversion.
5. Read the reglamento de condominio for letting rules and reserve fund position.
6. Check predial is current with a constancia de no adeudo.
7. For Centro Historico, confirm INAH heritage constraints before pricing any renovation.
8. Price comparable professional rents in pesos from live listings, not from the seller.

Costs and timings for the checklist above: the water review runs MXN 8,000-20,000 over about 5 business days, the escritura and libertad de gravamen search MXN 4,000-9,000 over 3-5 days, and independent legal review MXN 18,000-35,000. Total diligence lands near 1.5-2% of a $150,000 purchase.

Title work in Queretaro is comparatively straightforward and largely fixable. A development that cannot demonstrate water is neither, which is why this checklist is ordered the way it is.
